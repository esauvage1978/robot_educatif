An **ESP32 flashed with ESPHome** is a small electronic board with Wi-Fi (the ESP32) to which ESPHome, software that prepares its program from a simple configuration file, gives a precise role. Depending on what you wire to it, it becomes a sensor, a relay, a detector or a light visible in Home Assistant, the free software that gathers your connected devices. Each of the projects below has its own page: parts with quantities, wiring diagram, complete YAML file, steps with checks, Home Assistant integration and troubleshooting. If you are a beginner, start with a Beginner-level project and read the four basic guides first, which explain the words and gestures common to every build.

## Before you start: ESP32 or Zigbee?

It depends on what you are after. An ESP32 sensor can be customised endlessly (any I²C, SPI or GPIO sensor) and connects straight to Wi-Fi with no coordinator. A Zigbee sensor runs on a battery and goes anywhere, provided you own a coordinator. For a battery sensor stuck on a window, Zigbee is often handier; for a tailor-made USB-powered project, the ESP32 is more flexible. Price comparison depends on the seller: check it when you buy.

## Safety: 230 V mains is not a DIY project

None of the projects above asks you to touch 230 V: sensors run at 3.3 V, and valves and LEDs are powered by certified low-voltage blocks. A socket, a panel or a lighting circuit is a job for an electrician. If you would rather use a ready-made module to control a circuit, a certified relay is a safer option than a home-built one.
