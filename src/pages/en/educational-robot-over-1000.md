## What you already need to know

From 12 to 16, these machines only hold together with an adult or a teacher: assembly, software, and the safety of an arm or servos. From 16, someone who has already programmed or worked on mechanics can move ahead alone on part of the path. Students and engineers in training get the most from them, especially when the documentation talks about Linux, ROS, or OpenCV.

| Type | What you learn |
|------|----------------|
| **Humanoid** | A chain of servos, calibration, balance, gestures |
| **Arm** | Kinematics, gripping, the link between a camera and a grasp |
| **Mobile base** | Navigation, sensor fusion, sometimes SLAM depending on the configuration |
| **Quadruped** | Locomotion and stability, with a very strong visual effect |

The software pieces you meet, depending on the brand, are Python, vision (OpenCV, sometimes a detection model on the AI kits), and sensors such as a lidar, an inertial unit, or a camera. Technical English in the documentation is part of the prerequisite.
