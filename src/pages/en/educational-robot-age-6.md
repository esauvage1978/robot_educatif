**At 6, the best robot is the one the child can program alone in under ten minutes, without reading well.** In France this is CP, the first year of elementary school: reading is just starting, the wish to “do it myself” surges, and patience is still short. A robot that is too simple bores them in a week. A robot that is too technical ends up in the cupboard. This guide helps you aim between the two.

## What changes between 5 and 7

At **5**, the child chains two or three commands with an adult beside them (see our [robot guide for age 5](/en/best-robot-for-a-5-year-old/)). At **7–8**, they move on to Scratch blocks and the first sensors (see the [robot guide for ages 7–8](/en/best-robot-for-an-8-year-old/)). **Six is the bridge between the two**:

- **Longer sequences**: the child holds 5 to 10 commands in a row and starts to spot *where* the program went wrong.
- **First loops**: “repeat 4 times: forward, turn” becomes understandable when they see it on the robot.
- **Beginning reading**: pictograms, arrows, and colors are still essential. Software full of text discourages them.
- **Sessions of 15 to 25 minutes**: past that, it is better to stop on a success.

## The 5 criteria that really matter at 6

### 1. Programming you can read without reading

Arrow cards, buttons on the robot's back, colored blocks, or pictograms on a tablet: the child has to understand the interface **without asking you to read it for them**. This is criterion number one.

### 2. Screen-free, or a screen you control

**Screen-free** robots (coding with cards or buttons) are a good start, and they reassure parents who limit the tablet. A robot driven by an app can work, if the app has a “picture blocks” mode and you set time slots. Our [screen-free robot guide](/en/screen-free-educational-robots/) goes into that approach.

### 3. Little or no assembly

At 6, the wish is to **play right away**. A robot that is ready to use, or a build of a few minutes, is preferable to an 80-piece kit of screws, which stays a parent project.

### 4. Room to progress for two years

A good robot at 6 offers several levels: simple sequences, then loops, then conditions (“if you see an obstacle…”). Some models even move on to Scratch, which extends their life to ages 9–10.

### 5. A solid body and a way to recharge

Falls off the table, wheels pulled off, forgotten batteries: prefer a **USB rechargeable battery**, or plan rechargeable cells, and a closed chassis.

## Three mistakes to avoid

1. **Buying “for later”**: a build kit designed for age 10 stays in its box at 6, and the child keeps the idea that “code is too hard.”
2. **Mixing up a toy robot and a programmable robot**: a robot that dances or answers to a voice amuses them for a few days, and the child writes no program. Check that they can **create their own sequences**. Our guide to the [best programmable robot](/en/best-programmable-robot-for-kids/) explains the difference.
3. **Leaving them alone from day one**: the first two sessions are done together. Say it out loud with them: “You asked it to go forward three times, then turn. Shall we watch what it does?”

## Activity ideas for the first weeks

- **The mail carrier**: set out three “houses” (cups) in the living room. The child programs the path to deliver to each one.
- **The tape maze**: mark a corridor on the floor, then add one turn each day.
- **The dancing robot**: a sequence of quarter turns, forward, and back, repeated to music. It is a playful way to introduce the **loop**.
- **The mistake game**: you program a path with a deliberate error. The child has to find it and fix it. It is the best **debugging** exercise at this age.

## FAQ

### From what age can a child program a robot?

From 4 or 5 with button or card robots, with no reading required. At 6 a child can chain longer sequences and understand repetition. Scratch-style blocks become comfortable around 7 or 8.

### Is a robot with an app too much screen time at 6?

Not always. Programming a robot on a tablet is a making activity, very different from a cartoon. Set short slots (20 minutes) and alternate with screen-free robots or games.

### Does a 6-year-old need a robot they assemble themselves?

Not at 6. Building with a screwdriver becomes interesting around 8, with an adult. At this age the point is to program quickly and often.
