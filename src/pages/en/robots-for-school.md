## What the classroom asks for beyond the home

Twenty to thirty students handle the same fleet. Knocks are part of the session. A chassis in thick plastic, or in metal, can be repaired. Wheels and a distance sensor that unscrew save you from throwing the whole robot away. After-sales support you can reach in France, and a warranty of at least one year, are checked on the listing before the purchase order, not in a slogan.

The same robot should cross several years. In CM1–CM2 (the last two years of French elementary school, about ages 9–10), blocks and short challenges are enough. In 6e–5e (the first two years of collège, about ages 11–12), you add sensors and longer programs. In 4e–3e (the last two years of collège, about ages 13–14), text, often Python, takes over on a machine that allows it. A robot frozen at a single age forces you to buy the whole fleet again when the cycle changes. The age landmark, outside the school frame, is in [when to start](/en/when-to-start-coding-with-kids/).

With six to twelve robots, logistics count as much as the model. Identical chargers and a power strip avoid the session that starts with three empty batteries. A case or a dedicated cupboard has a place, not a corner of a desk. Updates are done in one pass when possible, and spare parts stay in a labeled bin, not in the drawer of the person who built the first robot.

The curriculum has to be able to host the session without a full rewrite. In technology, the landmarks published on Éduscol ask students to write a program, test it, and correct it. In lycée (French high school), NSI (Numérique et sciences informatiques) and SNT (Sciences numériques et technologie) have their own texts. The robot fits into a sequence that is already there, and assessment is on a skill from the common core, not on the brand of the chassis.

## Funding the case and making it hold

The money usually comes from several lines, not from a single pot: the school's equipment budget, a call from the region or the department, a grant from the rectorat (the regional education authority), a foundation (La Main à la pâte, corporate foundations), or the parents' association. Amounts change every year. This page does not republish ranges: the useful figure is the one in the current call, compared with the cost of the robots alone calculated above.

A case that goes through explains the project in skills and in a calendar, separates the robot price from the rest (training, maintenance, storage), and says who takes the fleet over the following year. A link with a local workshop or company helps, provided it is real.

To train without waiting for the hardware, M@gistère (the French teacher-training platform) offers paths that vary by academy, La Main à la pâte publishes resources from cycle 2 to cycle 4, and Éduscol puts sequence landmarks online. Makeblock provides paths for mBot and mBot2. On this site, the [one-hour projects](/en/mbot-one-hour-project-ideas/) cover a club or an AP hour (accompagnement personnalisé, a scheduled support period). Exchanges between colleagues happen on the Makeblock and Arduino forums, and in technology-teacher groups. The names of online events change: the useful core is a sequence already tested, not the social network.

## Running the session and the cupboard

With twelve robots and twenty-four students, stable pairs know which robot is theirs. You can rotate the machines from one session to the next so everyone sees the same sensors. One student in the pair explains while the other holds the robot, then you swap. Five minutes at the start and at the end are enough to count the cables and note a fault, if that time is inside the timetable and not “extra.”

The simplest storage is a rolling case with the robots, the cables, and a manual. A cupboard is justified only if the room is always the same. A number on each robot, and an incident sheet, let you know which one went for repair. A case with no label becomes a pile of cables by November.

The silly faults can be prevented. Batteries are charged at the end of the session, not on the morning you notice. One USB cable per robot, marked with the same color, avoids the cable “that sometimes works.” A grouped update once in the term is better than a fix in the middle of an assessment. Two wheels and a distance sensor in reserve cover ordinary breakage. Their price is checked when you order parts. It is not in the reading.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many robots for thirty students?

Ten to fifteen robots allow pairs or groups of three. Six robots force groups of about five, and a lot of waiting. The price follows the model: six mBots are 6 × 89.99 euros, or 539.94 euros of robots alone in the 6 November 2026 reading. Fifteen mBot2s are not deduced from a ready-made range. Calculate number × unit price, then price the storage separately.

### Do you need one computer per robot?

Not for mBlock. You write the program on one machine, then send it to the robot, often wirelessly. You need enough computers so groups are not queuing all period, not a computer bolted to each robot.

### Do brands mix?

Rarely. A Makeblock sensor does not screw onto an Arduino kit as if nothing were different. Choose one ecosystem, Makeblock, Arduino, or another range, and keep it for the length of the cycle. The [alternatives to LEGO Spike](/alternatives-lego-spike-comparatif-2026/) compare other fleets if the school is starting from there.

### How long does a robot last in class?

The warranty length is on the listing. After that, a fleet that is looked after (batteries charged, falls avoided, parts in stock) lasts several years in many rooms, without the manufacturer promising it. Batteries are the first part you replace.
