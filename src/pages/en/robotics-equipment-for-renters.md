## What can you set down without asking the landlord?

Your tenancy is the document that can limit noise and alterations. In England, the government overview of [private renting](https://www.gov.uk/private-renting) is the starting point; the agreement itself says what you may change. Works that alter the building need written permission. A robot on the floor, a USB lead and an extension lead you own do not alter the building. A rail screwed into the wall, a hole in the landlord’s table, or a module behind a light switch do. In the United States and in Canada, the lease is the text that applies. Read it before you buy. Do not treat a UK pound price as a local price there.

Peaceful use also means the neighbour. Small motors are audible in the room, and a party wall can carry the sound in the evening. The useful check is ten minutes of driving in the late afternoon, door shut, and a person in the next room telling you whether they hear it.

## How to choose

Four questions, in this order.

**Who is programming?** A child needs a result within the hour: the robot drives, a light changes. A teenager or adult who wants to understand a wire can spend an evening building. The age guides linked above cover that once the room is no longer the constraint.

**How much floor is really free?** Under a metre: Codey Rocky, a Penguin Bot or a board. A metre you can clear again the same evening: mBot or mBot2. A shared hallway in a house-share does not count. The robot stays in your room.

**How long are you staying?** Under six months, the entry price matters more than the sensor list. Over a year, mBot2 or a board you will keep using is worth the step up, because it moves with you.

**Whose network is it?** The classic mBot uses USB, Bluetooth and 2.4 GHz, and the [Makeblock product page](https://www.makeblock.com/products/buy-mbot) does not list Wi-Fi. In a building where the router is not yours, or behind a captive portal, USB is the reliable path. mBot2 adds an optional 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi radio. You can ignore it for the first programs. If a later project needs a network, a phone hotspot avoids opening a port on the landlord’s router.

## One evening, in order

1. Read the tenancy. Find the clause on alterations and the clause on noise. If a quiet hour is written down, the motors run before it.
2. Cover the landlord’s table with a mat you own. No screws in the top.
3. If the robot drives, clear about a metre by a metre. Pick up the lamp cable.
4. Put out a lidded box and a bag for screws. Label the box the first evening.
5. Plug your own switched extension lead into an existing socket. Switch it off when you leave for several days.
6. Follow the manufacturer’s build. Makeblock says about 15 minutes for the mBot. Standard-configuration sheets for Codey Rocky describe placing the body on the chassis.
7. Connect USB. For Makeblock, install mBlock with the [Windows guide](/en/install-mblock-5-windows/), then [first steps in the software](/en/first-steps-mblock-5/). The [first mBot program](/en/first-mbot-program/) makes the robot drive. For ELEGOO, the Arduino IDE and the maker’s examples, also downloadable from the official page, are the start. Follow the labels printed in the booklet that comes with the kit.
8. Watch the movement. The robot starts, then stops because the program says so, or because the ultrasonic sensor sees something if that sensor is in the program. An mBot2 at the published top speed takes about five seconds to cover a metre.
9. Pack it. Ten minutes, lid on, table bare, no adhesive on the paint. If the parts spill into a second box you might forget, the kit is too scattered for a move.

The next day, the same program should repeat the same run. That is the check that the cable, the battery and the screws are still together.

## Moving day

Take the AA cells out. For a lithium battery, follow the manufacturer’s sheet. If a carrier takes the boxes, lithium batteries have their own shipping rules: keep the robot with you, or read the carrier’s conditions before you hand it over.

Remove the robot from the building Wi-Fi if you joined it. The classic mBot does not have that step. mBot2 might. Take the USB cable, the microSD if you have a Pi, the booklet and the screw bag in the same box. Photograph the empty corner so you can compare it with the check-in photos. Nothing stays plugged in, and no new hole appears in the wall or the table.

## What to leave out

A resin printer and a laser cutter need ventilation, and a laser adds an eye risk and a fire risk. A rented home, often one room, is a poor place for either. Starting them belongs in a ventilated workshop.

An FDM printer sits on the table for hours, makes noise, and some filaments smell. You can take it with you, and it is still a poor first buy if the goal is to program a robot.

A large classroom brick set is awkward in a studio because of the piece count. See [LEGO SPIKE alternatives](/en/lego-education-computer-science-ai-2026-spike-prime-alternatives/).

A robot vacuum is a different purchase, floor and dock included: [robot vacuums](/en/smart-home/robot-vacuum-under-200/).

A soldering iron on the landlord’s table waits for a mat and an open window. Connector kits, and robots that arrive assembled, skip that step for the first month.

## When it stalls

**It will not drive on day one.** An mBot does not include cells. Buy them before the session, or the optional pack, and stay within 5 V / 1 A if you charge over USB. On Codey Rocky or mBot2, charge before you blame the motor.

**The neighbour hears the wheels.** Move the session earlier. Put the robot on a rug you own, so it is not bearing directly on the landlord’s floor. If it is still audible through a shut door, switch to a desk robot.

**The building Wi-Fi blocks the app.** Go back to USB. Bluetooth on the mBot and on Codey Rocky does not need the router. A phone hotspot covers a Wi-Fi trial without touching that router.

**A screw is missing at the next move.** The bag from evening one is the fix, plus one closed box with the cable inside.

**Check-out shows a box of wires.** If there is no hole and no adhesive, the box leaves with you. The photo of the empty corner is what you show.

**You wanted a workshop.** The tenancy and the room are not there yet. CyberPi, or a tabletop Arduino kit, teaches code and sensors until a home has room for a bench.

## FAQ

### Does a robot that packs away need the landlord’s written permission?

A robot set on the floor, or plugged into an existing socket, does not alter the building. Written permission starts when a screw enters the wall, the rented furniture, or the consumer unit. A stricter tenancy applies as signed. In England, start from the [private renting](https://www.gov.uk/private-renting) overview and the agreement itself.

### Does a stay under six months change the model?

Yes. The mBot at £53, or CyberPi at £47.20, keeps the sum small. A Pi plus a chassis is a later step: the chassis does not include the board, and the power supply is a separate buy. That pair makes sense when you already know you will continue after the move.

### In a house-share, where does the robot drive?

In your room, not in the shared hall. Measure the metre in your own space. Codey Rocky avoids a negotiation about the corridor. Keep the box in your cupboard so nobody treats it as rubbish on bin day.

### Does a robot vacuum belong in this guide?

Choose it on floor area, the dock and the door thresholds. The dedicated page is [robot vacuums under 200](/en/smart-home/robot-vacuum-under-200/). The dock leaves with you as well.

### What happens to the battery on moving day?

AA cells come out. A lithium pack follows the manufacturer’s sheet, and it travels with you if a carrier takes the other boxes. The mBot help centre caps USB charging at 5 V and 1 A.

### Does a captive portal stop you programming?

No, as long as you program over USB. That is the basic mode of the mBot, and it also exists on mBot2, Codey Rocky and CyberPi. The building Wi-Fi matters later, for a connected project, not for the first movement.
