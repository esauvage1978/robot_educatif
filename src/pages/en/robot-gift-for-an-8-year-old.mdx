## Why eight is a good age

At this age, a child connects a cause and an effect: if the sensor sees an obstacle, the motor stops. They handle a failed try better than at 6, and they can explain why they placed a given block. Free typing is still ahead. This is the age when a loop, a square on the floor, or a blinking LED means something.

A useful session often runs 20 to 40 minutes. That covers building or getting going again (about 15 minutes), a first program (10 to 15 minutes), then tests and fixes. At 6, that slot is often too long and tears come quickly. Around 10, many children already want to leave blocks for Python. Eight leaves Scratch in its place, with an adult beside them at the start.

School moves in the same direction, and the robot does not replace class. In CE2, multi-step problems ask for the same logic as a condition. In CM1, a slightly longer project can be shared, in class if robots are already there, or between siblings. The screen is where you write the program. The robot moves around the room, and the child can show it.

## What to look at

### The language and the build time

The first language here is a Scratch equivalent. Skip a kit that opens only onto the Arduino language, which is close to C++, and save written Python for the [guide for a 10-year-old](/en/robot-gift-for-a-10-year-old/). The ideal is a robot that starts in blocks and has a next step, so you do not have to replace everything the following year. On the mBot, that next step is mainly Arduino. Comfortable Python, in the same brand, is what the mBot2 offers.

The build matters as much as the software. Ten minutes and five parts leave little pride. Two hours and a box of two hundred screws discourage a first robot, especially on Christmas morning. Aim for 15 to 30 minutes, with a drawn guide the child can follow.

### Sensors, resources, and how long it lasts

Three sensors are enough to change the game. The ultrasonic sensor measures a distance and helps avoid an obstacle. The line follower keeps the robot on a track on the floor. LEDs show at once whether the program was understood. A buzzer for a tune, a light sensor, or an infrared remote stretches the ideas. They are not required on unboxing day.

A model already used in schools tends, in practice, to have tutorials, teacher sheets, a place to ask a question, and spare parts. French-language guides are part of that picture for families in France. The same robot can follow a line at 8, accept a more text-based language around 10 or 11, and take one more sensor later. The aim is three to five years of use, if the projects keep coming. That is not a span the manufacturer guarantees.

## What makes the gift fail

The most common miss is a kit that is too ambitious. A child who reads well is not automatically ready for a wiring build and the Arduino language. Scratch first. Text comes around 10 or 11, and a Raspberry Pi waits for the teen years. That is covered in the [gift for a teen](/en/programming-gifts-for-teens/). If you are unsure, step down one level.

A robot without a computer stays a box. Before you pay, check that a PC, a Mac, or a tablet can install the software. mBlock, for the mBot and Codey Rocky, installs on Windows, Mac, and Linux, and on an iPad from the 7th generation. Android sometimes works, with a more limited app. The USB cable is not always in the box. Some robots need Wi-Fi. Plan one to two hours with an adult for the first session. The gift does not sort itself out on day one.

Accessories change what comes next, not the robot's price. Rechargeable batteries save you from falling back on alkaline cells, which often last two or three hours. A track on the floor (black tape or a mat) and a Scratch book give ideas once the software examples are done. None of those prices is in the 6 November 2026 reading. Check them. The total with those extras is not calculated here.

### Prepare the parcel before the 25th

Opening the box in the morning, reading the manual for an hour, hunting for batteries, and installing the software often means a first try in the early afternoon, when everyone is already tired. If the mBot is the gift you chose, this prep avoids the stall.

1. Order while reading the delivery date on the listing. Aiming for before December 20 leaves a margin. After that date, only the delay shown on the listing counts.
2. When it arrives, install mBlock using the [first steps with mBlock 5](/en/first-steps-mblock-5/).
3. Put four AA batteries on charge the day before, and check the count on the box manual. Their price is separate.
4. Build the robot ahead of time, or reserve one to two hours on the morning of the 25th. The first connection is over USB.
5. Clear about one square meter on the table for the computer, and about two square meters on the floor so the robot can move.

For Codey Rocky or the Penguin, power follows that model's manual. If batteries or a cable are missing from the box, their price is not in this reading.

An educational robot is a different gift from a remote-control car. You learn before you play. The first course easily takes half an hour, and you start again several times. That is the game. If the child wants an instant result and nothing else, another gift will fit better.

## The first weeks

The first week can be three or four short sessions. You build, you plug in, you blink an LED. The next day the robot goes forward, back, and turns. Then a loop draws a square or a circle. Once the ultrasonic sensor is connected, the robot stops in front of an obstacle. You have succeeded when moving a block really changes the motion. If nothing moves, check the batteries, the cable, then the order of the instructions, before you decide the robot is broken.

The following week, four challenges are enough. A black tape line on the tile, a slalom between three obstacles, a tune (a birthday song or a seasonal one), then a timed course whose family record you keep. The [one-hour mBot activities](/en/mbot-one-hour-project-ideas/) spell out sessions of this kind.

Over the next two or three months, the same blocks become a small project: a guard that beeps when something passes the sensor, a delivery robot that follows a line and stops at a mark, a dance to a tune, a lap of the room that stays on the rug. The [ultrasonic sensor](/en/mbot-ultrasonic-1-measure-distance/) is the base for the guard and the slalom.
