**[LEGO](/en/lego-education-brand/) Spike Prime** and **Spike Essential** were reference kits, but **LEGO Education stopped selling them direct on 30 June 2026**. You now find them only at resellers clearing stock, at varying prices. Their last catalog prices (379 euros and 289 euros) are used here as a **comparison point**, not as a current offer. Euro amounts below are not converted to dollars. This guide compares the **strongest alternatives**: **mBot** (90 euros), **mBot2** (168 euros), **[ELEGOO](/en/elegoo-brand/)** (80 euros), **Fischertechnik** (150–300 euros). Savings, curriculum fit, equivalent projects. The buttons on this page open today’s listing.

## 1. Why look for an alternative?

### Last LEGO SPIKE catalog prices (before the stop on 30 June 2026)

- **Spike Prime**: 379 euros (high school or club) — no longer sold by LEGO direct
- **Spike Essential**: 289 euros (elementary or middle school) — no longer sold by LEGO direct
- **Extension**: 90–120 euros each

➜ **A class of 6 Spike Prime robots** at the last catalog price: 2,274 euros (extensions excluded)  
➜ **A class of 8 mBots**: 720 euros (a saving of **1,554 euros**)

### Other LEGO limits

- **Closed ecosystem**: LEGO-only extensions
- **Fragile bricks**: small parts go missing often
- **Proprietary software**: no transition to standard Arduino or Python
- **Renewal**: you have to follow the LEGO range

### What LEGO does well (worth keeping)

✅ Compatibility with household LEGO bricks  
✅ Solid complete sets  
✅ A polished Scratch interface  
✅ Recognition in schools

➜ **The stake**: find an alternative that is **just as educational** and **easier to afford**.

## 6. Hidden advantages of the alternatives

### Repair

**LEGO**: lost bricks mean ordering parts (delay, cost)  
**mBot**: standard screws, replaceable motors, often from a parts listing within a day

### Room to grow

**LEGO**: locked into the ecosystem (LEGO extensions only)  
**mBot**: works with Arduino, Raspberry Pi, and third-party sensors

### A path forward

**LEGO**: Spike, then what? (no clear next step after middle school)  
**mBot**: mBot, then mBot2, then Arduino, then Raspberry Pi (a natural progression)

### Community

**LEGO**: LEGO Education forums (mostly in English)  
**mBot**: the [Makeblock](/en/makeblock-brand/) community, including French-language help, plus Arduino (millions of users)

## 7. When is LEGO SPIKE still the better choice?

**Important**: since 30 June 2026, LEGO no longer sells SPIKE direct. For **new equipment**, check real availability at resellers before you decide. The cases below are mainly for schools **already equipped** with LEGO.

### Cases where LEGO still wins

✅ **A school already equipped with LEGO** (compatible bricks)  
✅ **Budget is not the constraint** (private school, a dedicated grant)  
✅ **Children under 8** (bricks are more intuitive than screws)  
✅ **The teacher has no technical background** (the LEGO interface is very guided)  
✅ **A LEGO contest** (FIRST LEGO League requires LEGO)

### Cases where the alternatives win

✅ **A tight budget** (equipping a class with 6–10 robots)  
✅ **Python or Arduino** is a clear goal  
✅ **IoT projects** (weather, notifications, home automation)  
✅ **Middle school or high school** (screws are fine, and you need room to grow)  
✅ **You maintain the fleet yourselves** (repairs by the teacher or the pupils)
