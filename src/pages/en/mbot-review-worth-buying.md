The **mBot** is often the first “real” programmable robot families consider after a screen-free coding toy. The question **“should we buy it?”** is not a simple yes/no: it depends on **age**, **adult time available**, **equipment** (Windows PC; tablets are sometimes limited) and the goal — a **short discovery** or a **multi-year project**.

This review is based on typical **family / club** use, common **mBlock** workflows, and the difference between the **classic mBot** and **mBot2**. For prices we give **indicative ranges**; always check the **product page** and **today’s price** before buying.

## 1. What the mBot really delivers (and what it does not replace)

### Recurring strengths

- **Complete robot**: movement, **distance sensor** (ultrasonic), **line following**, lights and **buzzer** — enough for varied challenges without changing brand.
- **Recognised teaching software**: **mBlock**, close to **Scratch**, with many **tutorials** and experience reports in English and French — see [install mBlock on Windows](/en/install-mblock-5-windows/), [first steps](/en/first-steps-mblock-5/) and [which mBlock to choose](/en/mblock-software-which-to-choose/).
- **Value for money** often **very competitive** versus more expensive “school” solutions, while remaining **repairable** and **expandable** (official accessories — [accessories guide](/en/mbot-accessories-where-to-start/)).
- **Possible path to text code** on **mBot2** (Python on **CyberPi**), without leaving the [Makeblock](/marque-makeblock/) ecosystem.

### Honest limits

- **Assembly and wiring**: this is not LEGO “clip” building; you must accept **screws**, ports and sometimes **re-plugging** after a drop.
- **First connection**: **Bluetooth** and **drivers** can take **30–90 minutes** the first time on some PCs — plan for a calm adult; see [Bluetooth troubleshooting](/en/mblock-bluetooth-troubleshooting/).
- **Child’s curiosity**: without regular small projects (tracks, music, robot duels), the robot can **gather dust** like any other tech kit.

## 2. Classic mBot or mBot2: the mistake to avoid

Many families hesitate between the two. In short:

| Criterion | mBot (classic) | mBot2 |
|--------|-------------------|--------|
| **Indicative budget (new, 2026)** | Often **around €80–100** (roughly **£70–90 / $85–110** depending on promos and pack — check local listing) | Often **around €150–190** (roughly **£130–170 / $160–210**) |
| **Age / level** | Very relevant **from ~8** with support | Often **more comfortable from ~9–10** if the goal is Python |
| **Pedagogical lifespan** | Excellent for **blocks** and classic **sensors** | Better for **rich projects** (LED matrix, mic, native **Python**) |
| **Software learning curve** | mBlock, gentle curve | mBlock + **power-up** without changing robot |
| **Movement precision** | Fine for school and home | **Encoders**: better for **precise tracks** |

**In one sentence**: if the budget is tight and the goal is a “**first serious Scratch robot**”, the **classic mBot** is often enough. If you aim for **several years** and early **text code**, **mBot2** avoids a quick repurchase. Details in [mBot vs mBot2](/en/mbot-vs-mbot2/).

## 3. Age band: when to say yes, when to wait

| Situation | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| **6–7 years**, little patience for tech | Prefer a **screen-free robot** or very guided workshops — [screen-free robots guide](/robot-educatif-sans-ecran-guide/) |
| **8 years**, interest in computers, adult available 1–2 h for setup | **mBot**: excellent **first step** |
| **10+ years**, robotics club or desire for Python | **mBot2** often **better value** over time |
| Teen who wants **free-form building** like **FIRST LEGO League (FIRST LEGO League (FLL))** / LEGO Technic | Compare with a **LEGO SPIKE** offer ([mBot vs LEGO comparison](/en/mbot-vs-lego-robot/)) |

To frame expectations by age for coding: [at what age to start](/programmation-enfant-a-quel-age-commencer/).

## 4. Real budget (beyond the box price)

Plan if possible for:

- **Rechargeable batteries** and a charger (heavy use).
- A spare **USB cable** if Bluetooth is unreliable.
- An **accessory** after 2–3 months (new sensor, competition pack) to **reignite** interest — [accessories by priority](/en/mbot-accessories-where-to-start/).
- A **book** or activity sheets (Scratch / robotics) if the child likes **paper** — gift ideas: [robotics & occasions](/idees-cadeaux-robotique-noel-rentree-anniversaire/).

If the budget is really limited: first read [cheap educational robot: the trade-offs](/robot-educatif-pas-cher-compromis/).

## 5. Verdict: “should we buy it?”

- **Yes, it is a good purchase** if you want a **Scratch-programmable robot**, with **real sensors**, a **documented ecosystem**, and a child **around 8 years minimum** (with a little adult help at the start).
- **Prefer mBot2** if you already know you want to **push the project** for 2–3 years and **introduce Python** without changing platform.
- **Prefer something else** (or wait six months) if the child has **no stable PC access**, if nobody can handle **setup**, or if you mainly want pure **LEGO constructionism** (see [mBot vs LEGO](/en/mbot-vs-lego-robot/)).
