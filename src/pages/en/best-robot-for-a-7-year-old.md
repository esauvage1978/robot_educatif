**At 7, decide between arrows and blocks before you look at brands.** In England this is often Year 2; in the US, 2nd grade. If reading is still hard, stay with the [6-year guide](/en/best-robot-for-a-6-year-old/). A screen-free robot is still a serious coding toy at this age: the child has to plan the whole path before pressing go.

A short build of about 15 to 20 minutes, with a screwdriver and you, can be part of the fun. A kit with tiny screws and a tangle of wires is still a parent's project. If the robot has to work on the morning it is opened, buy one that is already assembled.

An arrow robot is usually finished as a challenge within a year or two. A robot programmed in Scratch-style blocks, with a path toward Python, can stay in use well into primary school. That is the usual reason to spend more.

## What to check around the robot

Batteries are often not in the box. A tablet or a computer is required for Codey Rocky and for mBot. Bluetooth pairing fails more often than the coding does; plan one calm session for the setup, not for the gift opening.

## A simple decision

No screen, likes mazes: Botley. Ready for blocks and no interest in screws: Codey Rocky. Likes building and you have half an hour: mBot. If reading is still a struggle, do not force mBot; stay on arrows.

## Questions parents ask

### Is a screen-free robot a waste at 7?

No. Planning a full path before pressing go is the skill. A tablet adds sensors and sounds; it does not replace that.

### Should we skip mBot and buy mBot2 now?

Only if you already know they will move to Python and the budget is comfortable. At 7, mBot or Codey Rocky is enough. mBot2 is the subject of the [10-year guide](/en/best-robot-for-a-10-year-old/).
