**mBot2** is first of all a **mobile robot**: you think about a **path**, **sensors on the floor**, a **demo** you can run in a few minutes. **CyberPi** is a **development board**: you think about a **console**, **connected objects**, a **project with no wheels** if that is what you want. Both are [Makeblock](/en/makeblock-brand/) and both use **mBlock**, but the **teaching move** is not the same.

## 1. What the mBot2 does particularly well

- **Visible straight away**: the robot moves, avoids and follows. That is a strong way to **get a group or a family started**.
- **Scratch, then Python**, in a path that is often well documented for the mBot2 generation. See the [mBot2 article](/en/mbot2-makeblock-what-it-teaches/).
- **A story you can tell**: "our robot finished the path" is a goal you can **film** and that people who do not code can **understand**.

## 2. What the CyberPi does particularly well

- **Projects that do not need a course on the floor**: an interface, mini-games, sensors, **Wi-Fi**. See [CyberPi](/en/makeblock-cyberpi-overview/).
- A **maker** direction: a teenager who wants to **build** past a robot that only rolls.
- A **bridge** toward other **Makeblock** builds, or toward ideas that sit **closer to electronics** than to a game on its own.

## 3. Both in the same school year?

Some groups run them in sequence: **CyberPi** on the table for logic and for sensors that are more **abstract**, then **mBot2** when the point is a **moving** scene. At home, the budget often decides. Start from the **main need**: **roll and take on a challenge** (mBot2) or **experiment** on a board (CyberPi).

## 4. How this relates to Raspberry Pi

If a teenager mainly wants **Linux**, a **server**, or **Python away from a robot**, read [Raspberry Pi or a robot kit](/en/raspberry-pi-or-robot-kit-for-teens/). CyberPi and the **Pi** do not play the same role, even when the words "board" and "Python" show up for both.
