## Which profile

For a first discovery around 8–10, the mBot at 89.99 euros is enough: blocks, a short build, and the remaining budget goes to batteries if the manual asks for them, at their price that day. For a middle-school student aiming at Python, the mBot2 at 167.95 euros removes the step of a separate board. For a child of 10–14 who wants to transform the robot, the Ranger at 189.99 euros is the mechanical kit. In class, several mBots allow work in pairs; one or two mBot2 robots are enough if the teacher wants to show Python. All three boxes together serve a club. A second robot, six months or a year later, is a better plan than a grouped purchase on day one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the mBot2 replace the Ranger?

No. The Ranger has a chassis and a gyroscope meant for balancing and for changing shape. The mBot2 does not become that robot if you only change the program.

### Can an mBot become an mBot2?

Not fully. You can look at a CyberPi board, but the 59.99 euro kit in the reading is not priced as a graft into an mBot. The screen and part of the mBot2’s functions stay tied to that model.

### Do you need all three?

No, unless you are collecting or running a club. One model matched to the age and the language you are aiming at, then a second later if the interest holds.

The mBot at 89.99 euros remains the lowest ratio in the reading for a start. The mBot2 at 167.95 euros makes sense as soon as Python is the goal. The Ranger at 189.99 euros is for someone who wants three machines in the same box.
