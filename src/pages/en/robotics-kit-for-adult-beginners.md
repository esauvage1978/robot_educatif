**For an adult or a teen who is starting, the best robotics kit is a complete Arduino robot car with tutorials online: you build it in an afternoon and you really learn electronics and code.** Then, depending on what you enjoy most (wiring, Python, camera vision), you move on to the ESP32 or the Raspberry Pi. ## The three families of robotics kits

| Platform | Main language | Strength | Who it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arduino** (UNO, Nano) | C/C++ (Arduino IDE) | Simple, sturdy, a huge community | A complete beginner who wants to understand electronics |
| **ESP32** | C/C++ or MicroPython | Wi-Fi and Bluetooth built in, control from the phone | A beginner who wants a connected robot quickly |
| **Raspberry Pi** | Python | A real Linux computer: camera, vision, AI | A “code” profile who wants Python and vision |

In short: **Arduino to learn the basics**, **ESP32 for connected projects**, **Raspberry Pi for Python and the camera**. If you are hesitating between the last two, our article [Raspberry Pi or a robot kit?](/en/raspberry-pi-or-robot-kit-for-teens/) compares the two approaches.

## Criteria for choosing your first kit

### 1. The quality of the tutorials, before anything else

A beginner kit is worth mostly its **documentation**: a PDF or a site with wiring diagrams, commented example code, and a lesson-by-lesson path. The makers best known to hobbyists (ELEGOO, Freenove, SunFounder) publish their tutorials online and the code on GitHub. Check that they exist before you buy.

### 2. Everything in the box

Chassis, motors, sensors, board, cables, **and a battery holder**. Many kits do not include the 18650 cells or the batteries: read the product listing, or the robot will not roll on the day. Also plan a USB data cable (not a charge-only cable).

### 3. The level of assembly

- **No soldering** (connectors, screw terminals, ribbon cables): ideal for a first kit.
- **With soldering**: it trains you, and you add an iron, solder, and an afternoon of learning.

### 4. The language that motivates you

If you want to **code in Python** (data, AI, automation), start with a Raspberry Pi. If you want to **understand what happens in the wires**, Arduino remains the best school. Our [Arduino C track](/en/arduino-c/) and our [Python lessons](/programmation/python/) are free companions for either one.

### 5. The total budget

Count the kit, plus sometimes the batteries, a charger, and for a Raspberry Pi **the board itself** (often sold separately), a microSD card, and a power supply. A Raspberry Pi kit shown at an attractive price can therefore come back much higher than a complete Arduino kit.

## Your first projects, in order

1. **Blink an LED**: the “Hello World” of electronics, to check that the board, the cable, and the IDE work.
2. **Spin the motors**: forward, back, turn in place, then set the speed.
3. **Read a sensor**: display the distance measured by the ultrasound in the serial monitor.
4. **Combine**: “if an obstacle is less than 20 cm away, I reverse and I turn.” That is your first real autonomous behavior.
5. **Customize**: add a buzzer, a status LED, a line-following mode, or control from the phone.

Count an afternoon for the build, then one or two evenings per project. What matters is to **move forward in small tested steps**: when something breaks, you know it is the last change.

## Classic beginner mistakes

- **Forgetting the batteries**: check whether the 18650 cells are supplied, and whether a charger is included.
- **Choosing the biggest kit**: 200 parts and 40 sensors look impressive, and a kit you finish is worth more than a kit you abandon.
- **Copying the code without reading it**: take the time to change a parameter (speed, distance) and watch the effect. That is where you learn.
- **Reversing the power**: always connect the battery last and check the polarity. It is the number-one cause of burned boards.

## FAQ

### Can you learn robotics as an adult with no background?

Yes. The kits in this shortlist start from zero, with no soldering and no prior electronics knowledge. The only condition is regularity: a few hours a week are enough to have an autonomous robot in a month.

### Arduino or Raspberry Pi to start?

Arduino if you want to understand electronics and get a result quickly; Raspberry Pi if you want to program in Python and use a camera. Many makers start with Arduino, then add a Raspberry Pi for vision projects.

### Do you need to know how to solder?

Not for all the kits presented here, which use connectors and screw terminals. Soldering becomes useful later, for custom builds.
