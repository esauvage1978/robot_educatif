## Install the Arduino IDE and 7-Zip

The end of [OSOYOO lesson 1](https://osoyoo.com/fr/2021/02/20/sg90-servo-steering-robot-car-lesson-1-hardware-installation-and-servo-test/) asks for two free programs:

- the **Arduino IDE**, to write the code and send it to the board (I installed version **2.3.11**);
- **7-Zip**, to unzip the code files supplied by OSOYOO.

Both installations are **straightforward**: you download, click “Next”, and you are ready. No difficulty here.

## Open steering.ino and select the Arduino UNO board

OSOYOO provides a small test program, `steering.ino`, in the `steering.zip` archive of lesson 1. You unzip it, then open it in the IDE (**File → Open**).

![Arduino IDE 2.3.11 with the OSOYOO steering.ino sketch open: Servo library, servo on pin 9, FRONT value set to 90, then left, right and centre positions](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/ide-arduino-steering-ino-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

The code is short and readable, even for a beginner: the steering servo is on **pin 9**, the “straight ahead” position is set by `#define FRONT 90`, and the program sends the servo **left**, then **right**, then **to the centre**, with a one-second pause between each position.

Before uploading the code, you need to **select the “Arduino UNO” board** in the “Select Board” list, with the USB port that appears. The OSOYOO board is compatible with Arduino UNO R3: that is the choice that lets you upload the program.

![Arduino IDE toolbar with the Arduino UNO board selected](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/selection-carte-arduino-uno.webp)

## Upload the code with the battery unplugged

OSOYOO stresses this point, in red in the lesson: **switch off the battery** (or unplug the adapter) while you send the code. The board is then powered by the computer’s USB cable. You then click the **→ (Upload)** arrow.

![OSOYOO Sport Car V1.0 connected to the computer over USB: board’s blue LED on, voltage display, front sensor lit green](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/voiture-sous-tension-usb-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

Once it is powered, you can see the board’s **blue LED**, the **voltage display** and the **front sensor** lighting up green.

## The steering test: what it is really for

As soon as the code is loaded, the front wheels should turn **left**, then **right**, then come back **straight**. In my case, the test revealed **two tight spots** in the build. As I noted at the time: **I can see why the post-assembly test matters**.

### Tight spot 1: the tie rod was too tight

![Transparent acrylic steering tie rod of the OSOYOO Sport Car, with three round holes and an oblong hole at the end](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/biellette-acrylique-direction-osoyoo.webp)

At the acrylic **tie rod** of the steering, the load on the servo was **too high**: the servo was straining. I **enlarged the hole with a Dremel** (where the screwdriver is pointing in the photo) to give it some play.

![Close-up of the OSOYOO Sport Car steering: metal rod, white screwed part, black servo arm and tie rod](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/direction-servo-biellette-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

### Tight spot 2: the front-wheel screws

As during [assembly](/en/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-assembly-wiring/), you must **not overtighten the front-wheel screws**, or the wheels no longer turn and the servo cannot steer them.

**Tip**: if the servo strains or growls during this test, check these two places first. If the wheels do not come back quite straight, lesson 1 suggests remounting the servo arm or changing the `FRONT` value (increase it if the wheels veer left, decrease it if they veer right). Note that value: you will need it in the later lessons.

### The result

Once these points were fixed, the test passed: both **front wheels turn together**, left, then right, then back to centre. The movement is **abrupt**, which is normal: the program sends the servo straight to fixed angles, with no transition.

![OSOYOO Sport Car V1.0 from above during the steering test, sensors lit](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/test-direction-vue-ensemble-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

![OSOYOO Sport Car V1.0 during the steering test, front wheels turned](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/test-direction-roues-braquees-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

## Tip: the white button to replay the test

![White reset button on the OSOYOO Sport Car board, between the cables](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/bouton-reset-carte-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

You do not need to upload the code again to repeat the test: just press the **white button** on the board. It is the **reset** button: it restarts the program from the beginning, and the steering repeats left, right, centre. Handy for checking a setting after tightening or loosening a screw.

## The ESP8266 Wi-Fi module

![OSOYOO ESP8266 Wi-Fi module version 1.0 plugged in upright on the OSOYOO Sport Car motor shield](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/module-wifi-esp8266-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

Next step: I plugged in the **OSOYOO ESP8266 Wi-Fi module (version 1.0)**, standing upright on the shield. Check the **pin orientation** against the lesson diagram before you power it on.

According to [OSOYOO lesson 5](https://osoyoo.com/fr/2020/10/30/sg90-servo-steering-robot-car-lesson-5-wifi-udp/), this module lets you drive the car from a smartphone with the **“OSOYOO IoT UDP Robot Car”** app, either through your router (STA mode) or by turning the car into its own Wi-Fi access point (AP mode). The lesson asks you to have done lesson 2 (basic movements) first.

**Weak point noted**: the **white switch** that **turns the car on** has trouble **staying in position**. Check that it is firmly clicked in before each run, or the car may switch off mid-test.

## HC-02 Bluetooth pairing

![Android “Pair with HC-02?” screen asking for a PIN, with the note “Usually 0000 or 1234”](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/appairage-bluetooth-hc02-osoyoo.webp)

The kit also includes an **HC-02 Bluetooth module**. To pair it, go to the **phone’s Bluetooth settings**: the module appears under the name **HC-02**, and the phone asks for a PIN. Enter **1234**: that is the code that worked on our unit. There is no need to tick access to contacts.

[Lesson 6](https://osoyoo.com/2021/03/07/sg90-servo-steering-robot-car-lesson-6-mock-driving-with-bluetooth-app/) then uses the **“OSOYOO imitation driving”** app: you drive by tilting the phone like a steering wheel, with forward / reverse.

## Driving the car: several apps to choose from

OSOYOO offers **several apps** to drive the car from a smartphone, depending on the module in use and the driving style you prefer. I tried two of them:

- **OSOYOO IoT APP**: a full remote with a **directional pad**, **sliders** from 0 to 100 and **eight function buttons, F1 to F8**.
- **The steering-wheel driving app** (lesson 6, **Bluetooth** connection): a **steering wheel**, a **speedometer**, **Speed + / Speed −** buttons, an **Engine** switch and a gear lever for forward or reverse. It feels like sitting at the wheel of a real car.

![OSOYOO IoT APP screen: directional pad, sliders from 0 to 100 and function buttons F1 to F8](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/appli-osoyoo-iot-app-pilotage.webp)

![OSOYOO steering-wheel driving app: steering wheel, speedometer, Speed + and Speed − buttons, Engine switch, Connect button and gear lever](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-utilisation/appli-volant-bluetooth-osoyoo.webp)

## First driving attempt

The first driving attempt is **fairly tricky**: the car takes a little practice before you have it well under control. My tip: allow a **large clear space, with no obstacles**, so you can practise without stress (an empty garage, a large clear room or a yard).
