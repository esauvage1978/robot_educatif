Choosing an **educational robot in 2026** is not about “the best brand on paper”, but about **aligning three realities**: the child’s **real age** (motivation included), the **time** you can spend on setup and updates, and a **total budget** including accessories and batteries. **Scratch** here means a **block-based coding language** (you snap instructions together instead of typing syntax) — the usual entry path between about 7 and 12 on this site. **Python** is a **text programming language**: you type code on a keyboard; it usually needs **fluent reading** and some **typing**, and fits best from about **10–11** with support, or earlier if blocks are already easy.

## 1. Four family profiles (start here)

| Profile | Main need | Robot family to prioritise |
|--------|------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Discovery ages 5–7**, little screen time | Logic, sequences, physical play | [Screen-free robots (ages 4–7)](/en/best-robot-for-a-5-year-old/), coding games, simple board / mobile robots |
| **Light secondary ages 7–10**, PC at home | Scratch-style blocks, sensors, challenges | **Makeblock** (mBot…), sometimes **LEGO SPIKE Essential** if budget allows |
| **Passion ages 10–14**, long project | Scratch + text, varied sensors | **mBot2**, **Codey Rocky**, **LEGO SPIKE Prime**, boards like **CyberPi** |

## 2. Overview of families (strengths / limits / indicative budget)

### A. Screen-free robots & tangible coding

- **Interest**: lay foundations **without software friction** (no Bluetooth drivers, fewer updates).
- **Limit**: less “real code” exportable to secondary school; often a **ceiling** around 8–9 for tech-curious kids.
- **Budget**: often **€40–120** (check local Amazon listing) depending on brand and pack.
- **Lead**: [screen-free ideas for ages 4–7](/en/best-robot-for-a-5-year-old/) · [Matatalab Tale-Bot](/en/matatalab-tale-bot-educational-robot/) for story-led coding.

### B. “Scratch-like” robots on the Makeblock ecosystem (mBlock)

**mBlock** is Makeblock’s **free software** (blocks and sometimes Python) for mBot, mBot2 and Codey Rocky.

Examples: **mBot**, **mBot2**, **Codey Rocky**.

- **Interest**: direct link with what is often done in **clubs** or **after-school** activities; large community around mBlock.
- **Limit**: **first power-on** on Windows PC can be **demanding**; assembly is more “small programmable robot” than “big LEGO build”.
- **Budget**: **mBot** often **~€80–100**; **mBot2** often **~€150–190** (check local listing for £ / $); [budget trade-offs €200–500](/en/educational-robot-200-500-guide/).
- **Internal links**: [mBot overview](/en/mbot-my-first-educational-robot/), [mBot2 vs mBot](/en/mbot-vs-mbot2/), [CyberPi](/en/makeblock-cyberpi-overview/), [which mBlock to use](/en/mblock-software-which-to-choose/).

### C. LEGO Education — SPIKE Essential & SPIKE Prime

- **SPIKE Essential**: gentler entry, simpler hub, audience often **~6–9** depending on maturity; ideal for schools and families who want **classic LEGO + a bit of Technic** before the sprint to Prime.
- **SPIKE Prime**: hub with **6 ports**, motors and sensors compatible with **FIRST LEGO League (FLL)** — international school robotics league with LEGO robots — **Word Blocks** (Scratch 3) and **Python** programming; often aimed **from ~10** to handle complexity with autonomy. If SPIKE stock is unclear in your country, see [LEGO SPIKE alternatives 2026](/en/lego-education-computer-science-ai-2026-spike-prime-alternatives/).
- **LEGO Mindstorms Robot Inventor (51515)**: very complete consumer set but **discontinued new** at LEGO — interesting mainly **second-hand** if you accept end-of-life documentation ([LEGO product page for 51515](https://www.lego.com/en-gb/product/robot-inventor-51515)).
- **Budget**: **SPIKE Essential** often **~€150–250** depending on bundle; **SPIKE Prime** new often **~€300–400+** — above an mBot2 pack but with **bricks** and a very structured LEGO Education **curriculum**. Check stock ([SPIKE update 2026](https://education.lego.com/en-us/spike-update-2026/)).

Direct comparison with Makeblock: [mBot vs LEGO](/en/mbot-vs-lego-robot/).

### D. Boards and “almost robot” kits

**Arduino** means a family of **programmable microcontroller boards** (open source) wired to sensors, LEDs and motors; you usually program them in **C/C++** with the free **Arduino IDE**, for DIY builds rather than a ready-made toy robot.

Raspberry Pi (small **Linux** computer), microcontrollers and sensor kits: for families where an adult enjoys tinkering. Little “pretty all-in-one robot”, but a very high **ceiling** — see [Raspberry Pi getting started](/en/raspberry-pi-3-getting-started/) and [Pi 3 vs 4 vs 5](/en/raspberry-pi-3-vs-4-vs-5/).

## 3. Decision table (age × adult availability × budget)

Use this table as a **filter**, not an absolute law:

| Indicative age | Adult little available | Adult available + Windows PC |
|---------------|------------------|----------------------------------|
| **5–6** | Screen-free games, few parts | Reinforce with books / guided activities |
| **7–8** | Simple robot or short workshops | **mBot** or SPIKE Essential if budget |
| **9–10** | Packaged model with video tutorials | **mBot2** already relevant |
| **11–14** | Second-hand **FIRST LEGO League (FLL)** / Prime if club | **SPIKE Prime** or **mBot2** + Python |

**Expandability (summary)**:

- **LEGO**: expandability via “**bricks and competitions**” (**FIRST LEGO League**, combining sets).
- **Makeblock**: expandability via “**sensors + code**” (**mBuild**, Makeblock’s modular sensor line, plus accessories on Makeblock’s site).
- **Raspberry / board**: expandability via “**full system**”, but heavier adult teaching load.

### Mini case study: age 8, Windows PC, about €120

Apply section 1 + the decision table: an **8-year-old** with a parent available for setup and a **~€120** (or local equivalent) ceiling usually lands on a **classic mBot** pack, not SPIKE Prime and not a bare Raspberry Pi. Confirm the listing includes the robot (not only spare parts), install [mBlock](/en/install-mblock-5-windows/) before the first session, and keep one spare battery set.

## 4. Common mistakes in 2026

1. **Buying “too high-end”** for a first contact: a **robot left in the cupboard** costs more than a **cheaper kit that gets mastered**.
2. **Ignoring the software**: on **Windows or macOS**, check **mBlock** or **LEGO app** versions before the birthday, and plan an **installation evening** — [install mBlock](/en/install-mblock-5-windows/).
3. **Forgetting after-sales and batteries**: read **recent reviews** (not only the overall score) about connector breakage and battery life.

## 5. Troubleshooting after purchase (October 2026)

| Symptom | Common cause | What to do |
|---------|--------------|------------|
| Robot won’t connect over **Bluetooth** | Weak batteries, missing USB driver, outdated mBlock | Use **USB** for the first session; follow [mBlock Bluetooth troubleshooting](/en/mblock-bluetooth-troubleshooting/) |
| mBlock doesn’t see the robot | Wrong model selected, charge-only cable | Pick **mBot (mCore)** or **mBot2** in the device list; reinstall the CH340 driver if Windows asks — [install mBlock](/en/install-mblock-5-windows/) |
| Child loses interest after two sessions | Robot too hard or too “toy-like” for their age | Re-read the [guide for 8-year-olds](/en/best-robot-for-an-8-year-old/) or [screen-free ideas for ages 4–7](/en/best-robot-for-a-5-year-old/) |
| Missing parts in the box | Third-party seller, refurbished pack | Contact the seller within 30 days; compare the parts list on [Makeblock](https://www.makeblock.com/) or LEGO |
| SPIKE hard to find new | Some SPIKE sets no longer sold direct by LEGO | See [SPIKE alternatives 2026](/en/lego-education-computer-science-ai-2026-spike-prime-alternatives/) or buy a new **mBot2** |
