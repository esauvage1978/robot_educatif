Looking for a **coding robot for a child**? This guide compares three widely sold kits (Makeblock mBot, Makeblock mBot2 and the ELEGOO Smart Robot Car V4.0) using the **manufacturers' official spec sheets**, then helps you match one to your child's age. We have not lab-tested these robots: the figures below are the makers' own.

There is no single "best" robot for every family. What matters is your child's **age**, how much **adult time** you can give to the first set-up, the **computer** you have at home, and whether you want to stay with **blocks** (Scratch / mBlock) or move to **typed code** (Python, Arduino C++).

## 1. Decide on the goal before the brand

- **Playful discovery (around 5–7)**: short challenges, ideally little or no screen time.
- **Primary / elementary school robotics (7–10)**: Scratch-style blocks, several sensors, obstacle and line-following challenges.
- **Long projects and real code (10+)**: Python, data, cameras, or a single-board computer such as a [Raspberry Pi](/en/raspberry-pi-3-vs-4-vs-5/).

## 2. The three criteria that really matter

**Well-maintained software.** Without up-to-date tutorials and an active community, you are on your own at the first Bluetooth failure or Windows update. If you go with [Makeblock](/en/makeblock-brand/), start with our guide to [installing mBlock 5 on Windows](/en/install-mblock-5-windows/).

**Sensors and variety of challenges.** A robot that only drives in a straight line gets boring fast. Look for at least two kinds of challenge (distance, line, light, sound, display).

**Total cost, not just the sticker price.** Budget for rechargeable batteries, storage and possibly an add-on pack. Prices change often, so we don't quote them: check the current price at the retailer.

## 4. Quick pick by age

| Age (guide only) | Direction | Suggested kit |
|---|---|---|
| 6–8 | blocks, almost ready-built | mBot |
| 9–12 | blocks first, then Python | mBot2 |
| 13+ | typed Arduino code, wiring | ELEGOO Robot Car V4.0 or an [Arduino robot kit](/en/arduino-robot-kit-for-kids/) |

## 6. Common mistakes

- Choosing on today's price only, without reading recent owner feedback on durability and after-sales support.
- Underestimating the **adult time** needed for the first install (account, drivers, Bluetooth).
- Buying something too advanced as a first robot: a kit your child fully masters beats one gathering dust.

**Bottom line:** the best robot is the one that **gets used**, with clear software, varied challenges and an honest total budget.
