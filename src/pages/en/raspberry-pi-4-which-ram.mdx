> **Direct answer (October 2026)** — For a **first Pi 4** at home, school or for **Python** robotics, choose **4GB** in most cases. Pick **8GB** if you stack several **services** in parallel — for example **Docker** (software that runs apps in isolated “containers”), compile often or keep many browser tabs open. **2GB** suits **headless** network use. The **1GB** SKU is a low priority new in 2026 — compare a [Pi 5](/en/raspberry-pi-5-which-ram/) or a used **2/4GB** board instead.

**At a glance:**

| Variant | Typical projects |
| --- | --- |
| **Pi 4 2 GB** | Pi-hole, light server, kiosk, monitoring |
| **Pi 4 4 GB** | Linux desktop, Python, robotics, daily use |
| **Pi 4 8 GB** | Docker, compiling, large remote IDE, many tabs |

The **[Raspberry Pi](/en/raspberry-pi-brand/) 4 Model B** (2019, later revisions) is a **single-board computer** the size of a credit card. It differs from the Pi 3 especially with **two USB 3.0 ports**, independent **Gigabit Ethernet**, **up to 8GB of RAM** in **LPDDR4-3200** (soldered working memory, faster than on Pi 3) and **two micro-HDMI outputs**. This guide covers the **four common memory SKUs**, **how to choose**, the **accessory basket**, **software setup** and **troubleshooting**.

## 1. The four “RAM” variants

On the same **Pi 4 Model B** board, only the **amount of memory** changes; the **system-on-chip (SoC) BCM2711** (quad **Cortex-A72** ~**1.8 GHz**, 64-bit) is **identical**.

| Variant | RAM | Typical buyer profile |
|----------|-----|---------------------------|
| **1 GB** | 1 GB LPDDR4 | **Minimal** projects (sensor, small headless app). **Low priority** new in 2026 — a **[Pi 5](/en/raspberry-pi-5-which-ram/) 1 GB** (or richer used Pi 4) often replaces this SKU. |
| **2 GB** | 2 GB | **Light server**, **kiosk**, **monitoring**; **tight** graphical desktop (few tabs). |
| **4 GB** | 4 GB | **Sweet spot**: Raspberry Pi OS desktop, **Python** development, reasonable daily use. |
| **8 GB** | 8 GB | Several **Docker** stacks, compiling, larger remote **IDE** (integrated development environment), buffers for modest databases. |

All variants offer a **40-pin GPIO (General Purpose Input/Output — pins for sensors and add-ons)**, **CSI / DSI** ribbon interfaces (**CSI** = Camera Serial Interface; **DSI** = Display Serial Interface), **Wi‑Fi ac** and **Bluetooth 5.0**, with a **microSD** card as primary storage.

## 2. Specs that stop wrong projects

- **Video**: two **micro-HDMI** — plan **cables or adapters** (often **micro-HDMI → HDMI**).
- **USB**: **2 × USB 3** (fast drive / stick) + **2 × USB 2** (keyboard / mouse).
- **Power**: **USB-C 5 V** input; a quality **3 A (15 W)** PSU is wise if you power hungry peripherals downstream.
- **Thermals**: without a ventilated case or heatsinks, the **CPU** **throttles** (slows down to limit heat) quickly; for long load, aim for a **fan case** or **heat sinks**.

## 3. Which memory for which use?

**1 GB** — **systemd** scripts, **MQTT** (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport — lightweight messaging for sensors / home automation), small **Node** or **Python** without a GUI, reusing ageing stock. **Avoid** a desktop with a modern browser. For a **new** buy, compare the [Pi 5 RAM guide](/en/raspberry-pi-5-which-ram/) first.

**2 GB** — **Pi-hole**, **AdGuard**, light **reverse proxy**, home **Git**, small APIs. A desktop remains usable for **occasional admin**, not for a child juggling YouTube + online Scratch at once. For a **ready-made robot** instead of a bare board, see our [Arduino robot kit buying guide](/en/arduino-robot-kit-for-kids/).

**4 GB** — the **default recommendation** for a versatile **Linux** workstation, robotics with a heavier stack, or learning. Still a solid **price / comfort** compromise in 2026.

**8 GB** — more comfortable **Rust** / **C++** builds, **container stacks** such as **Docker**, modest **SQLite** or **PostgreSQL** home lab, or a desktop with **many tabs** if the SD storage stays fast.

## 4. Quick decision tree

1. **No monitor**, network scripts or server: **2GB** (or **1GB** if you measured RAM use).
2. **First Pi 4** for family / school / maker: **4GB**.
3. **Several apps** or **Docker** at once: **8GB**.
4. **Tight budget** but demanding new project: compare **Pi 4 8GB** vs **Pi 5 4GB** in the [Pi 3 / 4 / 5 comparison](/en/raspberry-pi-3-vs-4-vs-5/) before ordering.

## 5. Pi 4 or Pi 5?

If your budget allows a **Pi 5** with **active cooling** and a **good 5 A (27 W) charger**, the Pi 5 brings a **faster CPU**, **RAM up to 16GB** and **PCIe** (high-speed link to NVMe SSD HATs). Otherwise the **Pi 4 4GB** or **8GB** remains **highly relevant** — detailed table in the [Pi 3 / 4 / 5 comparison](/en/raspberry-pi-3-vs-4-vs-5/).

- **15 W USB-C PSU (3 A)** suited to Pi 4.
- **Fan case** or **heatsinks** for long loads.

## 7. Price: check with the seller

A **Pi 4** price depends on memory (2, 4 or 8GB), reseller and stock: verify it at purchase time. Also check whether the price includes an **SD card**, **case** and **PSU** — three items that inflate the total quickly if they are not “in the box”.

## 8. Software setup

The procedure matches other recent Pis: **Raspberry Pi Imager** on a PC, choose **Raspberry Pi OS** (Desktop or Lite), **SSH / Wi‑Fi / user** options in the gear menu — step-by-step: [Raspberry Pi 3 getting started](/en/raspberry-pi-3-getting-started/) (Imager steps are **the same** on Pi 4).

## 9. Troubleshooting: RAM, power and slowness

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Very slow system, tabs closing | **RAM** saturated (often 2GB with too many services) | Close apps; next purchase aim for **4GB** or **8GB** (RAM **not** upgradeable) |
| Thermometer icon or throttled CPU | **Cooling** insufficient | Fan case or heatsinks; avoid a bare Pi in a closed cupboard |
| Yellow lightning “under-voltage” | **PSU &lt; 3 A** or poor USB-C cable | Replace with a **15 W** PSU documented for Pi 4 |
| “Out of memory” when compiling or using Docker | SKU too small | Fewer containers, compile elsewhere, or buy **8GB** |
| Won’t boot | Worn microSD or corrupt image | Reflash with **Imager**; try a new **A2** card |

On Raspberry Pi OS, run `free -h` in a terminal — the **available** column shows real **RAM** headroom.

## Frequently asked questions

**2GB or 4GB to start?**  
In 2026, **4GB** for desktop or learning; **2GB** if the machine stays **headless** and light.

**Is a fan mandatory?**  
Not for a occasional sensor script; **practically yes** for compiling, 24/7 server or busy desktop.

**Can RAM be expanded later?**  
No — memory is **soldered**.
