Around **12 to 15**, two paths often meet. One is a programmable board such as the **[Raspberry Pi](/en/raspberry-pi-brand/)** (varied projects, Linux, Python on its own). The other is an **educational robot** (motivation right away, movement, sensors). They do not ask the same **adult time**, and they do not offer the same **level of independence**. Which one fits depends on what the teen wants to **show** first.

## 2. Choose a Raspberry Pi when…

- The teen wants to **host services**, dig into **networking**, a **camera**, the **shell**, or write **Python** past what a robot allows.
- You accept the **system** steps (SD card, updates). Our [Raspberry Pi 3 getting started](/en/raspberry-pi-3-getting-started/) guide is still a useful base, even though the models have moved on.
- You also want the [Pi 3 vs 4 vs 5 comparison](/en/raspberry-pi-3-vs-4-vs-5/) and [which Raspberry Pi 4 memory to buy](/en/raspberry-pi-4-which-ram/).

## 3. Choose a robot kit when…

- The stated goal is to **make something roll**, then **film** it or **show** the project to the family.
- You want **Scratch, then Python**, inside a **guided** environment: the [mBot2](/en/mbot2-makeblock-what-it-teaches/), or a **Makeblock** board, the [CyberPi](/en/makeblock-cyberpi-overview/), as a middle path. See also [mBot2 vs CyberPi](/en/mbot2-vs-cyberpi/).

## 4. The mix: a Raspberry Pi inside a chassis

Putting a **Pi** into a **robot** with motors and sensors is absorbing, and it takes **longer** to make stable than a robot that is ready to use. For a **first** purchase, decide from **patience** and from how much the teen likes **system tinkering**. The two worlds can **sit side by side** later, if the project lasts.

## 5. Short FAQ

**The teen wants both. Where do you start?**

Often the **robot**, if motivation is **low** or comes and goes. The **Pi**, if they already stay for long sessions in front of a tutorial.

**Does CyberPi replace a Raspberry Pi?**

No. CyberPi is a **Makeblock board** with the mBlock ecosystem. It sits close to Makeblock robotics. It is not a general-purpose Linux server.
