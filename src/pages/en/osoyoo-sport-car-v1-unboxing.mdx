![OSOYOO Sport Car V1.0 Arduino kit box in its shipping carton, with the Wi-Fi Control, Ultrasonic Detection, Obstacle Avoidance and Line Tracking icons](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-unboxing/boite-osoyoo-sport-car-v1.webp)

The box is clear about what you get: **“OSOYOO SPORT CAR V1.0”**, model **2021001900**, **ages 14 and up**, and four icons that sum up the programme: **Wi-Fi control**, **ultrasonic detection**, **obstacle avoidance** and **line tracking**. A link to the online tutorial is printed on the box itself.

When I opened it, my impression was good: **the packaging looks well done, every part is labelled and packed with care. It feels like quality.** For a kit you assemble yourself, that matters: you find each part without digging, and you are less likely to lose a screw straight away.

## What is in the box

![Foam tray from the OSOYOO Sport Car kit: 5-channel infrared line-tracking sensor, OSOYOO motor shield in UNO format, motor, four wheels with blue hubs, and the ultrasonic sensor bracket](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-unboxing/plateau-composants-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

The first layer is a **foam tray** that holds the main parts:

- a **5-channel infrared line-tracking sensor** (OSOYOO 5-Channel IR Tracker): it “sees” a black line on the floor;
- the **OSOYOO motor shield**, a board that stacks on the main board in **Arduino UNO** format and powers the motor;
- the **motor** that drives the car;
- **4 wheels** with blue hubs;
- the **ultrasonic sensor bracket**, for the sensor that measures distance to obstacles.

![OSOYOO Sport Car accessories: blue USB cable, jumper wires, bags of fasteners, USB-rechargeable 9 V 650 mAh lithium battery, steering cups and insulating tape](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-unboxing/accessoires-batterie-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

The second group is the accessories: a **USB cable** (to connect the board to a computer), **jumper wires** (to hook up sensors without soldering), **bags of screws and nuts**, the **steering cups** (the front steering parts), **insulating tape**… and, most usefully, the battery.

According to OSOYOO’s official parts list, the kit also includes the **OSOYOO board compatible with Arduino UNO R3**, an **MG90S servo** for steering, a second servo to aim the ultrasonic sensor, a **voltmeter**, an **ESP8266 Wi-Fi module**, an **HC Bluetooth module**, the two-plate **chassis**, and **tools** (Phillips screwdriver, hex driver, L-key). I will check every part when I assemble it.

### The welcome surprise: a rechargeable battery is included

This is the detail that makes a difference: the kit ships with a **9 V lithium battery that recharges over USB** (650 mAh, OSOYOO part 2020001400), **with its cables**. **You do not need to buy batteries** before you start, or replace them after every session. For a family or a club, that is both cheaper and less wasteful.

A useful reminder from OSOYOO: the voltage should stay high enough (above **7.2 V** in the lessons) for the car to drive properly; the voltmeter in the box is there so you can watch it.

## Why “Sport Car”? Steering like a real car

Most small Arduino robots turn by spinning the left and right wheels at different speeds (differential steering). The Sport Car uses a **steering rack driven by a servo**: the front wheels pivot, **as on a real car**, while one motor drives the rear wheels.

From a teaching point of view, that is interesting: you meet **mechanics** (linkages, steering angle) as well as electronics and code. The lessons explain, for example, that you should not go much beyond about **35°** of steering, or the front wheels drag and slow the car down.

## The six free lessons

| Lesson | What you learn | What you use |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Assembly and servo test** | Build the chassis, wire it, then centre the steering so the wheels point straight ahead | Servo, Arduino IDE |
| **2. Basic movements** | Forward, reverse, turns; set speed and steering angle in the code | PWMServo library |
| **3. Line tracking** | Follow a black line (2 to 3 cm wide) and adjust the sensor sensitivity | 5-channel IR sensor |
| **4. Obstacle avoidance** | Measure distance, stop, “look” left and right, then choose a path | Ultrasonic sensor on a servo |
| **5. Wi-Fi control** | Drive the car from a smartphone, through your router (STA mode) or as its own access point (AP mode) | ESP8266 module, “OSOYOO IoT UDP Robot Car” app |
| **6. Bluetooth driving** | Drive by tilting the phone like a steering wheel, with forward and reverse | HC module, “OSOYOO imitation driving” app |

The progression is logical: each lesson builds on the assembly from lesson 1, and you keep the same steering calibration value (`FRONT`) from one lesson to the next. It is a good way to meet **Arduino C** in small steps: you change a few lines (speed, angle) and see the effect on the car straight away.
