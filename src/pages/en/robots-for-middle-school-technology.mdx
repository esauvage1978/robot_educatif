In technology class, from about age 11 to about age 15, students have to write a program, test it, and fix it when the robot does not complete the course. In France that span is collège, or middle school, in four years: 6e (about 11), 5e (about 12), 4e (about 13), and 3e (about 14–15). This guide suggests machines and sessions for that expectation. It does not replace the landmarks on Éduscol, the official French curriculum site, and it does not turn a teaching choice into a requirement of the official bulletin.

Three unit prices were read on Amazon.fr on 6 November 2026. The mBot is €89.99, the mBot2 is €167.95, and the ELEGOO car V4.0 is €79.99. Eight mBots are therefore €719.92, and six mBot2 units are €1,007.70. Those are multiplications of the unit price, not bundles sold that way, and they include neither the batteries, nor the mats, nor the cupboard. Pack costing is in [equipping a classroom](/en/equip-a-classroom-robot-packs-2026/).

## What the curriculum actually asks for

In cycle 3, which for technology means the 6e year (about age 11), the student programs movements, uses a simple sensor, and tells a sequence from a loop. They have to be able to say whether their program succeeded. Blocks, in Scratch or in mBlock, are enough. The official text does not set a number of sessions. A progression you can hold uses six to eight of them in the year, inside technology time, not in place of the whole curriculum. Cycle 3 in France also reaches back into the end of elementary school; this guide starts at the middle-school year.

In cycle 4, the rest of middle school (about ages 12–15), you expect a structured program, with conditions and variables, then a project where sensors and actuators interact. In 5e (about 12), blocks remain the clearest medium. In 4e and 3e (about 13–15), Python becomes a good bridge toward high school (lycée). It is not written as the only language allowed. A class that stays on blocks can still assess the skills, as long as the algorithm is visible. Count rather on eight to twelve sessions if a 3e project fills the end of the year. That volume is this guide's, not a national timetable.

mBlock takes up Scratch's ideas: sequence, loop, and condition. It is not the editor published by the Scratch Foundation. In class, what matters is that the robot starts during the session. Ultrasonic, line following, and light are already on the chassis. The screws stay reachable, so you can replace a wheel without sending the whole robot back.

In 6e (about age 11), four activities are enough. A set course (forward, turn, wait) makes a sequence clear. Avoiding an obstacle adds a condition on distance. Following a black line adds a loop. Playing a tune on the buzzer reuses the sequence without the motors. The first program, step by step, is in [my first mBot program](/en/first-mbot-program/).

In 5e (about age 12), you reuse the same sensors with variables. A reversing radar changes the sound by distance bands. An intrusion counter increments a variable. A maze nests a loop and conditions. Eight mBots for a class, in groups of three, are 8 × €89.99, so €719.92 in robots alone at the 6 November 2026 rate. The classic mBot needs AA batteries, whose price you check separately.

## A four-year progression

The durations are class hours, to be fitted to the real timetable. The assessment suggested here judges the program and the demonstration, not the robot's brand.

In 6e (about age 11), eight hours are enough for a first contact. The first hour is the build: screws, motors, cables, and a robot that drives straight with the example program. The next hour fixes forward, turn, and stop. Two hours go to the ultrasonic sensor: read a distance, choose a threshold, trigger an alert. Two hours follow the black line, with a loop that corrects the path. The last two leave a challenge to a pair, starting from what has already worked. You assess by watching the session and with a short self-assessment: does the robot do the course that was announced?

In 5e (about age 12), ten hours structure the algorithm. One hour recovers the reflexes from 6e. Two hours introduce a variable: a counter, a score, a time. Two hours nest conditions, including an “and” and an “or.” Two hours pull out a reusable piece of program, the equivalent of a function. The last three build a maze the robot crosses without someone driving it by hand. The grade is on the program and on a demonstration, not on an oral.

In 4e (about age 13), ten hours move to text. Two hours show Python syntax and indentation, in mBlock, next to the equivalent block. Three hours retranslate a challenge already done in 5e. Two hours connect a sensor that had not been used, temperature or light. One hour displays the measurement on the CyberPi screen. The last two send information further, only if the middle school's network allows it. You can assess with a short questionnaire on the syntax and with a commented program.

In 3e (about 14–15), twelve hours hold a project. The first writes the need and the constraints. Two hours set the algorithm, on paper or as a flowchart, before the code. Six hours build, test, and fix. One hour writes instructions another group can follow. Two hours are for showing the robot and talking about it for five minutes. That oral practices speaking in public. It can feed the brevet oral, the French end-of-middle-school exam, if the teaching team keeps the project. On its own, it is not the exam.

## Four activities ready to launch

### At about age 11, a two-color light

The robot lights a red LED, waits, then a green LED, and starts again. In blocks, the structure is: on the flag, repeat forever, turn red on, wait three seconds, turn red off, turn green on, wait two seconds, turn green off. You assess the sequence, the loop, and the timing. Allow one hour, half of it to write and half to notice that the times are not the ones you expected. Block names in mBlock follow the mBot extension you installed. If they differ from this paragraph, the [mBlock 5 guide](/en/first-steps-mblock-5/) shows where to load that extension.

### At about age 12, a reversing radar

The ultrasonic sensor measures the distance. Beyond 50 cm, a green LED stays on and the buzzer is silent. Between 20 and 50 cm, an orange LED turns on and the beep is slow. Below 20 cm, the red LED turns on and the beep speeds up. The thresholds belong to the activity, not to a car standard. You assess multiple conditions and the use of a variable for the distance. Allow two hours, time to discover the sensor before you set the bands. What you should see: the behavior changes when you bring a hand closer, and it does not change if the sensor looks at the ceiling.

### At about age 13, the temperature on the screen

The program displays the temperature read by the CyberPi, then waits one second. The example below follows the API often shown in Makeblock examples. It was not replayed on an mBot2 for this update. If `get_temperature` or `show_label` is rejected, open mBlock's Python help for the version installed in the room. Function names move from one version to another.

```python
import cyberpi
import time

while True:
    temp = cyberpi.get_temperature()
    cyberpi.display.show_label(str(temp) + "°C", 16)
    time.sleep(1)
```

You assess importing a library, the loop, and the display. Allow two hours, especially if this is the group's first Python file. What you should observe: a number that changes when you warm the sensor with a hand, not a frozen piece of text.

### At about ages 14–15, a parking lot that counts

Two ultrasonic sensors, one at the entrance and one at the exit, increment or decrement a variable `places_occupees`, without going past `places_totales` or below zero. The screen shows the spaces left. A green or red LED repeats the same information for anyone who does not read the screen. This is a state program, not a one-hour exercise. Allow about six hours: a specification, trials with a single sensor, then both, then the display. You can see success when a person who did not write the code goes in and out, and the counter follows.

## Organizing 24 students and eight robots

Islands of three work: one student writes, another dictates or checks, the third places the robot on the course. Rotate the roles every 15 minutes, otherwise the same student keeps the keyboard. A color mark on the robot and on the group's sheet avoids the “first one there” argument. A visible timer beats a negotiation. The tracking sheet notes who completed the course. It is not a punishment. At the end of the hour, a laminated sheet (cable, remote, screws) and two students in charge of the equipment for the week are enough. Forgetting to put things away can cost five minutes of sorting at the start of the next session, announced in advance.

## Around the robots

The mBot needs rechargeable AA batteries, in enough sets that one lot charges while the other is on the robots. The charger and battery price is not in the reading. Check it. Do not replace that with a search for “batteries” that names no product. A black-line mat saves you from taping the classroom floor. Check that price too. Cardboard or book obstacles cost nothing.

mBuild extensions, for the mBot or the mBot2, are worth it only if the session already names them. One computer for two students is enough when the computer room is not free, and a projector is for showing a block before everyone clicks. An arena, a ramp, or mission scenery is comfort. They do not unlock the skill.

## Train yourself before the first session

Three hours let you avoid discovering the robot with the students. Half an hour to build it, half an hour in mBlock, an hour and a half on five challenges (forward, ultrasonic, line, and two variants), half an hour for Bluetooth that does not see the robot and for a firmware update. You have checked the troubleshooting when you find the robot in the device list, then see the example program start.

A two-day course, often offered in the regional training plan (in France, through the académie, the regional education authority), covers 6e and 5e first, then Python in 4e–3e. Enrollment is usually between June and September. Whether the course is free for the teacher depends on the académie. Check it. Do not enter it as zero in the budget automatically. Online, follow [mBlock 5](/en/first-steps-mblock-5/) and [the first mBot program](/en/first-mbot-program/). Makeblock tutorials and technology forums fill in, as long as you cross-check them with the curriculum, rather than copying a session that is too long for the class hour.
