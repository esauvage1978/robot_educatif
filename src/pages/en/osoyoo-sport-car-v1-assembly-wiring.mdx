## Which instructions to follow

I first watched the **official video**. It gives a sense of the finished car, but **it does not give the part references**. For the build, I therefore used [lesson 1: hardware installation and servo test](https://osoyoo.com/fr/2021/02/20/sg90-servo-steering-robot-car-lesson-1-hardware-installation-and-servo-test/), on osoyoo.com.

That lesson is the foundation for the later ones. It goes, in order, through:

- the **hardware installation** (chassis, motor, tie rods, wheels, boards, servos, sensors, battery);
- the **wiring** (motor, battery holder, voltmeter, servos and sensors on the motor shield);
- the **software setup** (Arduino IDE) and the **steering-servo alignment** (`steering.ino`, the `FRONT` value).

The detail of each screw and each connector is on that page: I am not inventing part numbers here. What comes in the box was covered in the [unboxing article](/en/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-unboxing/).

## What I noticed during the build

The assembly instructions are **reliable**, but **one tie rod is supplied as a spare**: you need **two**, and the box contains **three**.

On the **front wheels**, leave a **little play at the metal sleeve**: if you tighten it too much, the wheel will no longer turn.

I recommend having a **proper screwdriver** as well as the small one included in the kit, which is more comfortable for the flat-head screws.

I appreciated the **extra parts** supplied: if something breaks or gets lost, that is genuinely useful.

The **value for money** is already there on the **hardware**.

The site and the robot are really designed for **children** and for people **aged 14 and up**.

## The front wheels

The front wheels go on the metal rod of the steering (step 8 of lesson 1).

My tip: leave a little play at the metal sleeve, so the wheels turn freely, without forcing.

Once the wheels are on, the chassis is built: time for the wiring.

## The servos and the shield

![Shield mounted on its black bracket at the front of the OSOYOO Sport Car, blue SG90 servo for the ultrasonic head underneath, orange steering servo on the chassis, and orange, red and brown servo cables](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-assemblage/servos-shield-osoyoo-sport-car.webp)

In this photo, the **shield** is mounted on its **black bracket at the front**, the **blue SG90 servo** for the ultrasonic head is fixed underneath, and the **orange steering servo** is in place on the chassis, with its **orange, red and brown cables**.

Lesson 1 gives the official connections for these servos on the motor shield: the **MG90S** steering servo (orange) on **D9**, the (blue) servo on **D10**.

## The car once it is wired

![OSOYOO Sport Car V1.0 wired, side view: Arduino board and motor shield, yellow battery, 4-digit display, orange servo, and ribbon cables running forward to the sensors](/images/blog/osoyoo-sport-car-v1-assemblage/voiture-cablee-osoyoo-sport-car-v1.webp)

Once the wiring is done, you can recognise, from the side: the **Arduino board** and the **motor shield**, the **yellow battery**, the **4-digit display**, the **orange servo**, and the **cables running forward** (to the sensors).

The same lesson also describes the other official connections: remove the jumper caps on **ENB-D6**, **IN3-D9** and **IN4-D10**; connect the motor to **K1** or **K2**; connect the battery holder, the voltmeter, the ultrasonic sensor and the line-tracking sensor to the shield. The illustrated detail is on the [lesson 1 page](https://osoyoo.com/fr/2021/02/20/sg90-servo-steering-robot-car-lesson-1-hardware-installation-and-servo-test/).
