**At 4, buy a robot the child can run with two or three big commands, without a screen and without a screwdriver.** In England this is nursery or the start of Reception; in the US it is pre-K. The age on the box matters more than the school label.

A four-year-old can follow a short sequence ("forward, turn, forward"), likes an immediate result (a light, a sound, a robot that actually moves) and usually stays with a guided activity for about five to ten minutes. They learn by copying you. The robot is a reason to play together, not a lesson they do alone.

## What to look for

Choose a robot that works with **big buttons on the robot** or **thick picture cards**. A remote with more than a few keys is already a lot. The shell should survive drops. Button batteries must not be reachable by small fingers. The robot should stay on a mat or a floor grid, not shoot across the room.

Leave mBot, Arduino cars and Raspberry Pi for later. Those kits assume reading, a computer and an adult build. They are covered from the [8-year guide](/en/best-robot-for-an-8-year-old/) onward.

## Three kinds of robot that fit

| Kind | How the child codes | Screen | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Button robot (Bee-Bot family, Code & Go Robot Mouse) | A few buttons on the robot, then go | No | First sequences on a floor grid or a maze |
| Card robot (Matatalab Tale-Bot) | Physical cards in a row | No | A child who likes stories and placing cards |
| Anything with a tablet or a build | Blocks or parts | Yes | Not the default at 4 |

## Before you order

Plan to sit on the floor with them. Show one path, let them copy it, then change a single step when the robot misses. Sessions of a few minutes are a success.

Check whether batteries are in the box. Many of these kits do not include them.

## Mistakes

Buying a "real coding robot" with an app because it looks more educational. At 4 the app becomes the activity, and the child waits for you to fight Bluetooth.

Buying a kit with small screws. The first success should be a robot that moves, not a build that you finish alone.

## Questions parents ask

### Does a 4-year-old need a tablet?

No. Buttons or cards are enough. A tablet is a later choice, from about 6 or 7, and only if you want it.

### How long will they use it?

A button robot stays interesting while the paths get longer. Many children move on around 6. That is normal; it is not a failed purchase if the first months are playful and short.
