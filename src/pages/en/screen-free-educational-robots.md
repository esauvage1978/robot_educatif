## Which one by age

Around 3 or 4, a session often lasts five to ten minutes and the adult stays nearby. The Tale-Bot Pro matches the 3–5 listing when the child likes the path to be told as a story. The Code & Go kit or the mouse alone fit if buttons on the robot are enough and the budget stays the lowest of the four. The Bee-Bot fits on a grid, in class or at home, with the same short instructions. The step-by-step for this band is in [a robot for a 4-year-old](/en/best-robot-for-a-4-year-old/).

At 5, the child more often lasts ten to twenty minutes and starts to correct an order with help. Botley 2.0 is the model whose listing explicitly says "screen-free" and "5 years and up." Code & Go and Bee-Bot stay right if the maze or the grid is already a pleasure. The frame is in [which robot at 5](/en/best-robot-for-a-5-year-old/) and in [coding: when to start](/en/when-to-start-coding-with-kids/).

At 6, Botley 2.0 remains the most complete screen-free choice on this list. Code & Go still works as a reminder, with shorter challenges. The [age-6 guide](/en/educational-robot-age-6/) compares this step with robots that do use a screen.

Around 7, many children read a simple instruction and last twenty to thirty minutes when the challenge is clear. A screen-free robot still forces you to plan the whole path before you press. It gets short when the child wants to reread the program, add a condition or show it on the computer. The shift is described in the [age-7 guide](/en/best-robot-for-a-7-year-old/).

## What "screen-free" actually changes

None of these four robots needs an account, a Bluetooth pairing or an update to play the first time. The sequence is in the room: you see it, you say it, you change a press or a card. It is the same breakdown as later in Scratch ("first this, then that"), on another medium. If the session starts only once the tablet is on, the robot no longer answers the original request.

The mBot (ASIN B07XR98YYQ, €89.99 the same morning, [Check today's price on Amazon.](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07XR98YYQ?tag=roboteducatif-20)) and Codey Rocky (ASIN B07CGH8NZ7, €149.99, listing 6 years and up, Scratch and Python, [Check today's price on Amazon.](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07CGH8NZ7?tag=roboteducatif-20)) are not in this choice: they are programmed on a screen, in mBlock. They become relevant when reading and the computer are already accepted. The comparison is in [a programmable robot with Scratch](/en/scratch-programmable-robot/) and getting started is in [mBot, a first robot](/en/mbot-my-first-educational-robot/).

## Before you buy

The age written on the listing is a floor, not a promise. Also look at the size of the Code & Go pieces, the floor space for Botley, and the mat for the Bee-Bot. The first successes should be short: two or three orders, a robot that arrives, then you stop.

Cards and walls get lost faster than the robot. A box with compartments avoids the session spent hunting the "turn" arrow. Accessories (mats, outfits, new courses) renew the play; they are not in the prices above, which are those of the named robot or kit.

In class or in a club, the robot can be the character that has to deliver a message. You agree the order of the cards before you start. Later, the same sequence is rewritten as blocks on the board: the link with Scratch is then explicit. The landmark [Scratch at school or at home](/en/scratch-school-home-getting-started/) is for that moment, not for unboxing day.
