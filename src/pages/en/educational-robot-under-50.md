The Penguin Bot is the only mobile robot in the reading under $55. It walks on two legs, plays sounds, and is programmed like an Arduino project. The [ELEGOO obstacle-avoidance tutorial](https://wiki.elegoo.com/Robots%26Kits/Penguin-obstacle-avoidance) describes an ultrasonic sensor for the distance in front of the robot and two infrared sensors on the sides. Music goes through the speakers in the build. The same tutorial explains how to program that avoidance. The visible project is walking, avoidance, and a short sequence of moves.

The listing is aimed at ages 8 and up. At 8, an adult installs the Arduino IDE, chooses the board, and uploads the first program. The child can then change a wait, a servo angle, or a melody, and check that the robot does something else. If nobody is there to open a code file, the mBot's blocks will be less steep, for $30 more in the reading (around $75 against around $45).

Assembly takes an afternoon: small screws and servo direction, which the ELEGOO tutorial spells out. It also shows a case plugged into a battery port. Depending on the listings, you sometimes read a USB-rechargeable battery, sometimes cells. This reading does not include that accessory. Check the price of the format written on today's listing, and do not add it by guesswork to the around $45.

The step-by-step documentation is first ELEGOO's, in English on the wiki, with videos. It is not the French mBot corpus. The [ELEGOO brand](/en/elegoo-brand/) page already says so: the Penguin is a small budget, not the default classroom robot. If nothing moves, go back over the USB cable, the battery port, and the servo direction before concluding that the board is at fault.

## Buying a used robot

A used mBot can cost less than around $75. This reading does not give that price. You compare it with today's new price, not with a round figure kept from memory.

1. Have them film the robot moving forward and reacting to an obstacle or a line.
2. Compare the listing's contents with the manufacturer's list: sensors, cable, wheels, remote if there is one.
3. Check that mBlock or the Arduino IDE can still be downloaded for that exact model.
4. Set aside a listing at the new price, or a listing with no video of the robot running.

A used Penguin is judged the same way: both legs move, the ultrasound responds, the USB cable is in the bag. A missing part is negotiated. You do not guess it from a photo of a closed box.

## Which purchase fits the situation

If you want to know whether robotics appeals, and an adult can upload an Arduino program, the Penguin Bot at around $45 is enough. If it appeals, the mBot or the ELEGOO car can come later. If it stays in a cupboard, the sum lost is the one in the reading, not a total of batteries and books that this page does not know.

If the child already has an mBot and wants something else, the Penguin walks instead of rolling. It is not a second course on the floor. The [Makeblock brand](/en/makeblock-brand/) page recalls what the mBot already covers.

If the child is 8, starting from zero, and around $75 are possible, do not stay under $55 on principle. The [gift for age 8](/cadeau-robot-enfant-8-ans/) starts from the mBot, because blocks avoid C syntax on the first Sunday. The [$110 guide](/en/educational-robot-under-100/) compares that mBot with the ELEGOO car.

If the interest is electronics more than the robot, the UNO kit at around $50 is clearer than a biped whose code nobody will open. Under age 8, leave this page: Code & Go and Bee-Bot are above $55 in the reading.

A remote-controlled robot with no software, a “robot” box that mentions neither blocks nor Arduino, or software announced as paid after the purchase, do not answer the title of this guide. The robot's price is the project's price only if the power supply named on the listing is already at home. Otherwise, check the price of that power supply separately.
