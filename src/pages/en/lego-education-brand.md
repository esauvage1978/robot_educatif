<h2 id="history">History and where LEGO Education fits</h2>

<p>After years of <strong>Mindstorms</strong> and <strong>WeDo</strong>, LEGO Education focused on <strong>SPIKE</strong> before announcing in 2025/2026 that SPIKE would leave the direct catalogue. The brand stays strong in <strong>schools</strong> (class packs, teacher training) and in families chasing <strong>FIRST LEGO League</strong>. Last catalogue figures we use in comparisons — Essential around <strong>$320</strong>, Prime around <strong>$415</strong> — are **reference points**, not guaranteed UK or US shelf prices today.</p>

<h2 id="ranges">SPIKE, WeDo and BricQ ranges</h2>

<h3 id="spike">SPIKE Essential and SPIKE Prime</h3>
<p><strong>SPIKE Essential</strong> (set <strong>45345</strong>) targets entry level: simpler hub, Word Blocks, guided primary models. <strong>SPIKE Prime</strong> (<strong>45678</strong>) adds ports, longer missions, <strong>Python</strong> in the app, and <strong>FLL</strong> alignment. Both use <strong>Technic</strong> building logic.</p>

<h3 id="wedo">WeDo 2.0</h3>
<p><strong>WeDo 2.0</strong> remains the classic early-primary kit: Smarthub, tilt and distance sensors, tablet app with block coding.</p>

<h3 id="bricq">BricQ Motion and BricQ Prime</h3>
<p><strong>BricQ</strong> lines push <strong>physical science</strong> (forces, motion) with bricks and printed investigations; some boxes skip a connected hub, others slot into a wider LEGO Education pathway.</p>

<h2 id="ages">Ages, classroom and home use</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Ages 6–8</strong>: WeDo 2.0 or SPIKE Essential with an adult; short sessions. See <a href="/en/best-robot-for-an-8-year-old/">best robot for 8 years</a>.</li>
<li><strong>9–12</strong>: SPIKE Prime or Essential depending on budget and FLL. Compare <a href="/en/mbot-vs-lego-robot/">mBot vs LEGO robot</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Class sets</strong>: expensive at scale; many schools keep legacy SPIKE. Hub: <a href="/en/best-educational-robot-2026/">educational robot guide</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Home</strong>: plan **roughly an hour of building** before the first program; keep the app updated on tablet or PC. For an instant rolling robot, read <a href="/en/makeblock-brand/">Makeblock</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="software">LEGO Education software</h2>

<p>The <strong>LEGO Education SPIKE</strong> app (tablet, Chromebook, Windows) offers <strong>Word Blocks</strong> then <strong>Python</strong> on Prime. <strong>WeDo 2.0</strong> uses its own app. If your child already lives in Scratch, see <a href="/en/mblock-vs-scratch/">mBlock vs Scratch</a> on the Makeblock side.</p>

<h2 id="faq">LEGO Education FAQ</h2>

<h3>What is LEGO Education for a child?</h3>
<p>Technic kits with a programmable hub: SPIKE, WeDo, or science-focused BricQ sets.</p>

<h3>SPIKE Essential or SPIKE Prime?</h3>
<p>Essential from about 6–10; Prime for middle school, Python, and FLL.</p>

<h3>Can you still buy new SPIKE?</h3>
<p>Not from LEGO direct since 30 June 2026 — reseller stock only.</p>

<h3>Which app should we use?</h3>
<p>LEGO Education SPIKE app; WeDo 2.0 has its own app.</p>

<h3>LEGO Education or Makeblock for a class?</h3>
<p>LEGO for building and competitions; Makeblock to equip a class faster for less.</p>
