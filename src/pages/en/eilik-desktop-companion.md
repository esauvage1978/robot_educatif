**Eilik** belongs with the small desktop companions: it reacts to touch, shows animations on a small screen and offers short games or “mood” behaviours. This page says what that is for. It is not a robotics kit and it is not a programming course.

This page has no product photo and no shop price. The price is read on the listing of the exact model, on the day you look.

## What it is useful for

| Aim | What a companion of this kind often does |
| --- | --- |
| Start | Talk about tech before opening an IDE |
| Name | Inputs (touch, a choice), outputs (sound, lights, faces), a loop of reactions |
| Compare | A character that reacts, against a project whose logic you write step by step |
| Pace | A 15–20 minute station before code, for example the [scrolling Scratch floor](/en/scratch-video-game-scrolling-backdrop/) or [first steps in mBlock](/en/first-steps-mblock-5/) |

## Who it is for

Roughly 8–14, a club, a library table or a family gift that starts a conversation about sensors and interfaces. One session can open a longer course. It does not, by itself, cover a year of “robotics and code”.

Four short activities, with no extra hardware:

1. **Interview the robot** (10 minutes). Groups ask “what does it do when…?” and write the words: input, reaction, rule.
2. **Two columns** (20 minutes). One column for Eilik, one for another object already in the room. The point is to say the purpose, the interactions and who it is for.
3. **Reaction or instruction.** Describe “it does X when I do Y”. List what is playable at once, and what would need a program written elsewhere.
4. **No screen required** for the youngest. The same wish to discover by handling shows up on other robots; the [mBot and Codey Rocky comparison](/en/mbot-vs-codey-rocky/) is the programmable side.

## What it does not replace

Open programming, an obstacle course designed by the pupils, or a term of sensors and briefs. One Eilik can be the demonstration on the table. The rest of the group needs something each pupil can repeat: [Scratch](/en/scratch-activity-square/), [mBlock](/en/first-steps-mblock-5/), or a robot you chose for the class.

## Questions

**Does it need a computer?** Often no, to play or to show it. A coded project over the year uses a computer somewhere else.

**Is it enough for a secondary coding course?** Not as the only tool. It can be the reason to start.

**How is it different from a school robot?** Eilik puts the weight on a character and ready-made reactions. A classroom robot puts the weight on writing the behaviour, in blocks or in text. Both can sit in the same year. They are not the same job.
