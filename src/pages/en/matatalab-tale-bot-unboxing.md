## Short tutorial: first steps (5 minutes)

1. **Charge** the robot with the supplied cable (wait for a steady light before the first session).
2. Place the **language board** under the robot: choose **FR** and the distance (**10 cm** or **15 cm** per step).
3. Press **Program**, then chain **Forward × 2**, **Back × 1**, **Dance** — confirm with **Play**.
4. Try **Story** mode (for example, a walk at the zoo). See the [Tale-Bot FAQ](/en/matatalab-tale-bot-faq/) if the voice stays in English.

I want to thank Matatalab for giving me the chance to try their educational robot. They were kind enough to send it to me free of charge so I could test it independently, with no influence from them.

## Unboxing and first look at the Tale-Bot

The robot arrives in 2 boxes. My son and I opened the first one, which holds the robot. He was so excited that we did not have time to read the manual, including the part about switching it to French ([this article has help for that](/en/matatalab-tale-bot-faq/)).

During that first look, we had it run a few instructions: forward, back, turn left, and turn right. What he liked most was making it dance.

While my 4-year-old was getting the hang of it, I looked up how to switch it to French. The documentation is not very clear on that point. In fact it is so simple that I went looking for something complicated.

### What is in the box

The 2 boxes contain:

*   The Tale-Bot and its power cable
*   Different arms (wings, arms, and mounts compatible with Lego bricks and felt-tip pens)
*   3 costumes (a rabbit, a police officer, and a little boy)
*   1 booklet of repositionable stickers (not tested, because I know my son will stick them everywhere except on the boards)
*   1 setup board for the language and the step size (10 cm or 15 cm)
*   Interactive pages
*   and the manuals

### Story activity

As soon as we switched the Tale-Bot to French, we tried Story mode, starting with the walk at the zoo. My son was captivated by that story and listened to it at least six times in the space of an hour. He was also curious about the tale of the frog and the sunflower seed, and he listened to that one carefully too. The stories on the Matatalab Tale-Bot Pro are impressive, and they are an excellent way to encourage a child's learning and creativity.

In day-to-day use, at 3 years and 10 months, he is fully independent for this activity. It is simple and suited to that age.

### Counting and shapes activity

My son also loves the counting activity on the Matatalab Tale-Bot Pro, where he has to find a given number of strawberries, six for example. He can count the strawberries, so he completes this activity easily. He did have to grasp one small subtlety: when you "offer" a solution, you must press the **Play** key to check how many strawberries were found, and that took him a little while to get used to. This activity is an excellent way to stimulate a child's logical and mathematical thinking, while still feeling like a playful game.

### Costume activity

My son takes a lot of pleasure in dressing the Tale-Bot in one of the three costumes. He can change the accessories by himself, which builds his sense of independence and his creativity. Those accessories do seem fragile, though, and they are already starting to wear out. That is a shame, because he loves changing costumes often.
