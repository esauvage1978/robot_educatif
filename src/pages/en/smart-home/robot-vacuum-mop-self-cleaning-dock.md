> **Quick answer (November 2026)** — For most homes with hard floors and rugs, choose **rotating mop pads with auto lift** plus a dock that **washes and dries** pads if budget allows. On a tight budget, a **drag pad** still helps daily—see [robots under $200](/en/smart-home/robot-vacuum-under-200/). This guide sits in our [smart home hub](/en/smart-home/).

“Vacuum and mop” appears on almost every new robot, but the hardware varies wildly. A damp cloth dragged behind the bot is not the same as twin **spinning pads** (discs that rotate under pressure). Here is how to read the specs—and what docks add.

## Floors

- **Tile, sealed wood, vinyl:** all systems OK; rotating pads and rollers handle dried marks better.
- **Oiled or waxed wood:** minimal water, lowest flow; check floor manufacturer advice.
- **Homes with rugs:** insist on **automatic pad lift** or a dock that removes pads before carpet zones.

## Upkeep

A dock cuts work but does not remove it: bags, filters, brushes, pads and dirty tanks need care. Before buying, check **consumables availability** in your country and reachable support. **Home Assistant** (open-source smart home) can track wear when the integration exposes it: [robot vacuums in Home Assistant](/en/smart-home/robot-vacuum-home-assistant/). Navigation tech (**LiDAR** = laser mapping, **SLAM** = simultaneous localization and mapping): [LiDAR, camera or gyro](/en/smart-home/robot-vacuum-navigation-lidar-camera-gyro/).

## FAQ

**Are rotating pads worth the price?**  
Yes if you mop weekly on tile or vinyl; skip the wash dock if you only need vacuum on mostly carpet—see [navigation guide](/en/smart-home/robot-vacuum-navigation-lidar-camera-gyro/).

**Will a drag mop replace manual mopping?**  
It spreads light dirt; sticky kitchen stains still need a human pass occasionally.
