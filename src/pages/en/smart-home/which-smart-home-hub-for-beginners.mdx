You want to automate your home, but you wonder whether you need a central box, and which one. That box is called a smart home hub (or controller). It plays the role of conductor: it receives what your sensors say and decides what to do, for example turning on a lamp or switching off a radiator.

Two names come up all the time: Home Assistant, free software that gathers all your devices in one place, and Homey, a ready-made box you buy off the shelf. You will also run into Zigbee, a low-power radio used by battery sensors, and Matter, a common language that more and more brands understand.

By the end of this page you will know which option matches your project, how much to plan for a start, and which traps to avoid. There is no "best" hub for everyone: there is the one that suits your goal, your home and your taste for tinkering.

## The answer at a glance

| You want... | The most realistic option |
|---|---|
| To start locally, be able to grow, and not pay for the software | **Home Assistant**, on a Green box or a Raspberry Pi 5 |
| A box that works almost straight away, with few settings | **Homey Pro** or **Homey Pro mini** |
| Open-source software with a plugin catalogue | **Jeedom** |
| To control a few objects by voice, without installing anything | **No hub**, just the brands' apps or the voice assistant you already use |
| To stay in the Samsung world | **SmartThings**, which often relies on the internet and on a hub |

For most readers who want to automate without a mandatory subscription, Home Assistant is the most complete choice. For something that "just works", Homey is simpler. And to test without investing in a box, you can start with Wi-Fi smart plugs and bulbs, then move to a hub later.

## Choose by goal

### Heating and energy savings

You need two families of devices: sensors, which measure temperature (and sometimes consumption), and devices that act, such as thermostatic radiator valves, small heads that screw onto the radiator to regulate the heat. Home Assistant excels here: it keeps a history of temperatures and allows very fine rules. Homey also drives valves through its apps.

Without a hub, brand thermostats (tado, Netatmo and others) work well, but they often lock you into their ecosystem.

### Security and alerts

You need contact sensors for doors and windows, motion detectors, and possibly a camera. A local hub has one advantage: it can sound a siren or turn on the lights as soon as the door opens, even if the internet is down. Before buying a camera, check that it can work locally with your hub; some Reolink models do, for example.

### Lighting

Without a hub, Wi-Fi bulbs or plugs, or Matter-compatible ones, do the job. With a hub, you can use Zigbee bulbs, such as those from IKEA or Philips Hue, or Shelly Wi-Fi modules that are controlled locally. Start simple: five different apps for five lamps is the surest way to get tired of it.

### Blinds and roller shutters

They often need a module slipped behind the switch or inside the shutter box. This is homeowner work, with an electrician if you are not qualified. If you rent, choose a different goal to start with.

### Comfort: presence and ambiences

A presence detector can turn on the light when you arrive or start some music. There are two types: the classic infrared detector (PIR), which reacts to the heat of a moving person, and the radio-wave detector (mmWave), which senses even someone sitting still on the sofa. Home Assistant and Homey both handle them very well.

### Renting, no drilling

Plugs, battery sensors, buttons and thermostatic valves need no tools. Our page on [smart home for renters](/en/smart-home/smart-home-renters-no-drilling/) covers all of this. A hub such as the Home Assistant Green or Homey plugs in without drilling anything.

### Tinkering and personal projects

Home Assistant on a Raspberry Pi or a mini PC, combined with ESP32 boards (small programmable electronic boards with Wi-Fi) and the ESPHome software (used to program them), gives maximum freedom. The flip side: more maintenance. You will find ideas in our [ESP32 project ideas](/en/smart-home/esp32-smart-home-project-ideas/).

## Comparing the options

| Option | Who is it for? | Works without internet?* | Difficulty | Common trap | Starting budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Home Assistant Green** | A beginner who wants a plug-in box | Yes, everything stays at home | Low to medium | Forgetting to buy the Zigbee stick for battery sensors | Box alone is mid-range; more with stick and sensors. Check the retailer |
| **Home Assistant on Raspberry Pi 5** | A tinkerer who likes to understand | Yes | Medium | Weak power supply, unreliable storage card | Depends on the kit (Pi, power supply, storage), plus the Zigbee stick |
| **Home Assistant on a mini PC** | Someone recycling a machine or wanting more power | Yes | Medium | Power use, noise, choice of drive | Very variable, from a spare computer to a new machine |
| **Homey Pro or Pro mini** | Someone who wants an all-in-one box without tinkering | Yes, for locally supported devices | Low | App catalogue to check, proprietary hardware | Often the most expensive of the list; check locally |
| **Jeedom** | Someone who wants open-source software with plugins | Yes | Medium | Some plugins are paid, learning curve | Depends on the box or Raspberry Pi chosen, plus plugin budget |
| **SmartThings** | A Samsung user | Partly | Low | Dependence on the account and on Samsung's servers | Check by hub and offer |
| **No hub** | A curious person who just wants to test | Often no | Very low | Several apps, few rules across brands | A few plugs and bulbs |

\*"Works without internet" means locally controlled devices keep working, while online services such as voice control stop.

These are orders of magnitude, drawn from retailers and manufacturers' documentation, without any fixed price. Always check the current price with the seller on the day you buy.

## Jeedom

Jeedom is open-source software with an active community. It works with add-on modules called plugins, some of which are paid on the official store. It is a good choice if you like a plugin-based system with documentation. Before buying a Jeedom box, read the official documentation to spot the plugins you will need.

## SmartThings and voice assistants

SmartThings (Samsung) and the voice assistants from Google, Amazon (Alexa) and Apple make voice control and Matter compatibility easier. For advanced scenarios, or for everything to keep working without internet, though, they are more limited than a Home Assistant or a Homey running at your place. Many homes therefore keep them as a complement: Home Assistant can "present" its devices to them so you can control them by voice.

## Smart home without a hub

It is possible, and very useful for testing: one or two Wi-Fi plugs that measure consumption, a Matter-compatible bulb, and off you go. The limit is that devices from different brands do not talk to each other, and they often depend on the internet. When you get a taste for it, bring everything together in Home Assistant or Homey instead of piling up apps.

## Where to start, and what to pick to grow

If you want to start tomorrow without tinkering, take Homey or the Home Assistant Green. If you want to start locally and learn, take the Home Assistant Green, or a Raspberry Pi 5 with a Zigbee stick. For maximum room to grow, Home Assistant is the most open: it accepts a huge number of brands, ESPHome boards, and the MQTT messaging system that tinkerers use a lot.

Three traps come up often. The first is a mandatory cloud (meaning operation that requires the manufacturer's servers) that is not clearly announced: read the product sheet before buying. The second is a subscription required for basic functions: check what works locally. The third is incompatibility between a device and your hub: cross-check the [compatibility table](/en/smart-home/compatibility-matrix-home-assistant-homey-zigbee-matter/) with the manufacturer's documentation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Home Assistant free?**
The Home Assistant software is free. The hardware (a Green box or a Raspberry Pi, a Zigbee stick) is paid separately. An optional subscription called Nabu Casa makes remote access easier, but nothing forces you to take it for a setup that stays entirely at home.

**Does Homey replace Home Assistant?**
For many everyday uses at home, yes. Home Assistant stays more open for DIY, ESPHome and rare integrations. The safest approach is to compare your list of devices with the Homey app catalogue and with the list of Home Assistant integrations.

**Can I change hub later?**
Yes, especially if you chose devices that speak open languages such as Zigbee or Matter. Devices that depend entirely on their brand's servers are harder to migrate. The [compatibility table](/en/smart-home/compatibility-matrix-home-assistant-homey-zigbee-matter/) helps you check this before buying.

**Is SmartThings suitable in the UK and Europe?**
Hubs and devices are sold country by country, and Matter improves compatibility. Check availability where you live, the plug type and voltage, and what keeps working without internet.
