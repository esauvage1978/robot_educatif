Renting does not stop you having a connected home. The rule is simple: **anything that plugs in, sticks on or screws into an existing fitting** can go in and come out with you. Anything that touches the consumer unit, built-in switches or the boiler usually needs the landlord’s written OK—and often a qualified electrician.

**Our starter pick for renters: a Zigbee USB stick on Home Assistant, plus Zigbee plugs and sensors.** Nothing is fixed to the wall; on moving day the whole system fits in a box. Dongle details: [best Zigbee dongle for Home Assistant](/en/smart-home/best-zigbee-dongle-home-assistant/).

## Low-risk kit

**Smart plugs.** Ideal first step: schedule a space heater, measure load, remote off. Respect the maker’s max rating (often 13 A in the UK, 15 A circuits in the US) and avoid daisy-chained extension leads. See [smart plugs for the UK and US](/en/smart-home/smart-plugs-uk-us/).

**Battery sensors.** Temperature, humidity, door, water leak: stick or place them. Choosing models: [Zigbee temperature sensors](/en/smart-home/zigbee-temperature-sensor/). Use removable adhesive to protect paint.

**Wireless buttons.** A Zigbee button by the door can turn off all lamps on the way out—no wiring.

## Check before you buy

**Smart TRV heads.** On a wet radiator with a thermostatic valve body, you unscrew the head and swap in a smart one—no draining. Keep the old head for hand-back. Check thread compatibility with the supplied adapters.

**Cameras.** Indoor cameras are fine technically. In the UK, filming shared stairwells or the public pavement is restricted; point the lens at your own rooms only ([ICO guidance on home CCTV](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/cctv/). In the US, state and HOA rules vary—avoid pointing at neighbours’ property or public sidewalks where local law restricts recording.

## Leave for the landlord

In-wall modules, motorised blinds wired into the house, consumer unit work, hard-wired boiler stats: these alter the property. Get written permission and use a registered electrician (Part P in England and Wales; licensed electrician and local code in the US).
