I had already set up the [Homey Pro](/en/smart-home/homey-pro-unboxing-setup/) at home, with the idea of using it first to **open and close my garage door**. That part is now done: a **NodOn SIN-4-1-20** Zigbee relay (Multifunction Relay Switch, firmware 3.13.0-3.1.4) drives the original push-button, and an **Aqara MCCGQ11LM** door sensor reports whether the door is open or closed.

The motor is a **Tubauto ProCom** (part 4512433) on a **sectional door**. A wall push-button was already wired to a pair of spring terminals on the board. I did not replace that button: the relay simply closes the same contact, in parallel.

This wiring was done in France, on a 230 V supply (brown on L, blue on N). If you are not comfortable with mains voltage, ask a qualified electrician. If you would rather use an ESP32 and ESPHome, I have already written up another route: [open a garage door from a smartphone](/en/smart-home/garage-door-smartphone-esp32-esphome/). Here everything goes through Homey and Zigbee. For the protocol vocabulary, see [Zigbee, Wi-Fi, Thread or Matter](/en/smart-home/smart-home-protocols-zigbee-wifi-thread-matter/).

## The kit

- the **NodOn SIN-4-1-20** relay (one channel, dry contact, no energy metering);
- the **Homey Pro**, already running;
- the **Aqara MCCGQ11LM** door and window sensor;
- a 230 V mains lead with a plug;
- a weatherproof junction box, fixed to the ceiling next to the motor's socket.

## Safety, before you touch any wires

The module is supplied at **mains voltage**. I **isolated the supply** for the whole of the wiring, and I only plugged it back in once the terminals were tight, with no bare copper showing. If that is not familiar work for you, have an electrician do it.

I also advise against a flow that would **close the door on its own**, with nobody watching what is underneath. A notification, then a manual confirmation, is safer.

## The wiring

The NodOn is powered by a mains lead with a plug, in the motor's socket: **blue on N**, **brown on L**. The module sits in a **weatherproof junction box**, fixed to the ceiling next to that socket — a garage is not a living room, and the box keeps the module off the open air.

<Photo src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/nodon-dans-boitier.webp" alt="NodOn module installed in an open junction box, with the mains leads and dry-contact wires already connected" caption="The NodOn in its box, once the wiring was finished." />

The relay's **dry contact** (the two terminals on the right, with the relay symbol) is wired **in parallel** with the push-button terminals: its two wires go into the **same spring terminals**, next to the button's own wires. After that, the original button still works — I tested it again before closing the box.

<Photo src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/carte-moteur-procom-bornes.webp" alt="Tubauto ProCom motor board, showing the spring terminals of the wall push-button and the unused neighbouring pair" caption="The ProCom board: the relay joins the original button terminals, not the neighbouring pair." />

On this board, a **neighbouring pair of terminals** was left unused. It may be a safety input or a photocell: I did not connect the relay there, because I did not have the manual to hand.

## Pairing in Homey

In the Homey app I added the device through the **NodOn** app, choosing **Multifunction Relay Switch** — the "1 RELAY" model without energy metering, not the "METERING" variant. In the French app the same device is labelled "Interrupteur Relais Multifonction".

<Photo portrait src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/homey-choix-appareil-nodon.webp" alt="Homey app screen listing NodOn devices, with the multifunction relay switch selected" caption="The right choice in the NodOn app: the multifunction relay switch." />

To put the module into pairing mode, a **long press** (about five seconds) on the **PRESS** button, with an insulated tool, is usually enough: you release when the LED flashes. A module that has never been paired can also enter that mode on its own for a few minutes after power-up.

A useful anecdote: a **short press** on PRESS operates the relay. During the test, the garage door opened. That is not a fault — it is exactly what the button is for. I closed the door with the original push-button, then started the Homey search at the moment of the long press.

## Pulse mode, with no extra flow

Once the device was added, the advanced settings offered **Pulse mode duration**. **0** turns the mode off; the range is 0 to 65,535. I set **1,000 ms** (one second). It works perfectly: each activation mimics a press on the button, and I did not need a flow that turns the relay on, waits, and turns it off.

<Photo portrait src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/homey-parametres-avances.webp" alt="Homey advanced settings screen for the NodOn relay, with pulse mode among the options" caption="The module's advanced settings in Homey." />

<Photo portrait src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/homey-impulsion-1000ms.webp" alt="Homey setting Pulse mode duration set to 1000 milliseconds" caption="1,000 ms: a one-second pulse, like the original button." />

If the motor had not reacted, I would have tried 1,500 ms. That was not needed.

The NodOn also acts as a **Zigbee router**: it stays powered and strengthens the mesh, which is welcome in a garage a little apart from the rest of the house.

## The Aqara sensor

To know whether the door is open or closed, I paired an **Aqara MCCGQ11LM** (Door and Window Sensor) in Homey, through the Aqara app. The sensor body is mounted **at the bottom, on the rail** of the sectional door; the **magnet is on the door**, lined up with the sensor when the door is closed.

<Photo src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/capteur-aqara-rail.webp" alt="Aqara door sensor fixed at the bottom of the sectional garage-door rail, with the magnet on the door panel" caption="The Aqara sensor at the bottom of the rail, the magnet on the door." />

I tested an opening and a closing before tidying up and closing the box.

## The “Garage open > 10 min” flow

The relay moves the door. The sensor is there to warn you. I built a flow **Garage open > 10 min**:

1. **When** the contact alarm turns on (the door opens);
2. **And** the contact alarm is on;
3. **Then** a push notification “The garage door is open”, **after 10 minutes**.

<Photo portrait src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/homey-flow-garage-ouvert.webp" alt="Homey flow Garage open for more than 10 minutes, with an opening trigger, a condition and a delayed notification" caption="The alert flow, as it stands in Homey." />

I did receive the test notification.

<Photo portrait src="/images/domotique/porte-garage-nodon-homey/notification-porte-ouverte.webp" alt="Homey push notification saying the garage door is open" caption="The notification received on the phone." />

There is still a limit, and it should be stated clearly: the **And** card is evaluated **when the door opens**, not after the 10 minutes. If you close the door in between, the notification still goes out, unless you add a **second flow** that, on closing, **cancels the delayed actions** of the first one. In my Homey I have not yet pinned down the exact name of that card: look for it in the Flow group, around the word for “delay”. That fuller flow will come in a separate article.

## Going further

This first setup is enough day to day: a pulse from the app, open or closed status, and an alert if the door stays open. A **more advanced flow** — cancelling the notification if you close the door in time, perhaps an evening reminder — will be described later, on a dedicated page. No automatic closing without anyone watching, for the reasons already given.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the original button still work?**
Yes. The relay is in parallel on the same terminals. After the wiring, I tested the wall button again: it still moves the door.

**Do I need a flow to mimic a button press?**
Not with this module in Homey: **pulse mode at 1,000 ms** is enough.

**Can I use the terminals next to the button?**
I did not. On this board, the neighbouring pair may be a safety input. Without the ProCom manual, the safer option is to parallel the button.

**Can the door close by itself after 10 minutes?**
I deliberately left that out. The alert warns you; closing stays a confirmed action, because nobody sees what is passing under the door.
